# Decisions

Running record of what we've settled and why, so the reasoning survives between sessions.
Newest thinking at the top of each section. Last updated 2026-09-30. Loaded into every new Claude Code session through CLAUDE.md.

## What this app is

- **A seating chart, attendance and points tool that runs *alongside* Gynzy / NaniBox / Classroom Screen** — not a replacement for them. Gynzy already does whiteboard, lessons, timers and noise meters well; its seating chart and attendance are weak. That gap is the product.
- **Target user: teachers who want marble-jar dynamics but can't have the jar.** Derek's room runs on a physical marble jar, a per-class jar, and a battery wall that fills toward a treasure chest. Colleagues like watching it work and default to ClassDojo instead because they can't replicate it.
- **Deliberately fewer features than ClassDojo**: no parent accounts, no behaviour taxonomy, no setup. The things people complain about are the things we leave out. That's a position, not a limitation.
- **The name is Class? Yes!**, the call-and-response every teacher already uses to get the room: the teacher calls, the whole class answers. It is a whole-class moment, which is what the app is for. Written ClassYes wherever punctuation can't go (web address, app stores, search); classyes.app is the address to buy, and classyes.com is a premium domain worth buying only once teachers are paying. Names with "jar" were ruled out because ClassJar, Jar of Stars and Classroom Screen's behaviour jar already exist; Class Go, Class Ready, Ready Class and Ready to Learn all clash with products or programmes that exist. The phrase is too common to trademark on its own.
- **The logo is drawn in code, not a picture.** Two chalk speech bubbles, "Class?" in white and "Yes!" answering in yellow (`ClassYesLogo.tsx`), redrawn from a Gemini sketch. As an SVG it stays sharp on any board, and the words are live Andika, so the `a` is the one the students write. It is the splash screen's headline, with "Welcome, Teacher!" moved under it as a smaller line. The 2+2=4 doodle moved left to make room, because the taller headline pushed the button down on top of it.
- **The mascot is the splash screen's chalkboard, cheering**: wood frame, green board, a chalk smile with its eyes shut, both arms thrown up for the "Yes!" and a few stars. The artwork is the teacher's own, made in Gemini and traced to SVG in Canva. The whole character is the browser-tab icon (`public/favicon.svg`); the close-up tile (one arm, the frame's corner) is the home-screen icon (`apple-touch-icon.png`), on its own green because iOS fills see-through corners with black. A simpler redraw was made for tab size first and replaced by his: at 32px his reads fine, and at 16px any tab icon is a coloured square with a face. The speech bubbles stay as the name on the splash; the mascot is the face of the app where there is no room for words.
- Because it's designed to share the screen rather than own it, **coexisting with the lesson app is the core premise**, not a nice-to-have.

## Parked — revisit, don't rebuild

- **Marble sounds** (`design/marble-sounds.html`, published as its own artifact). Four candidates mapped to classroom moments. Derek: "gets so close to the actual sound." Undecided whether they go in the app; the synthesis ports straight into `lib/sound.ts` if so.
  - The insight behind it: his marble swirl is a *command* (it changes the room's behaviour), where all our current sounds are *feedback* (they confirm something happened). The award sound should carry across a room, not confirm a click.
  - Attention-getter sound boards already exist (Classroom Screen, Class123), so a sound *library* is commodity. What isn't commodity is the reward system's own sound being the cue.
- **Bamboo hit** (`design/sounds/bamboo-hit.mp3`, one of the recordings the teacher sent for the minus sound). A short wooden "tok". He loves it and wants it kept for a use we haven't found yet. It sits where a board speaker is strongest (mostly 400-1000Hz), and was recorded near full volume: about 22% puts it just under the coin.
- **Electron port** — deferred, and now only polish. The floating class goal (below) stays on top of full-screen PowerPoint and video on the real board, so the browser already does the part that mattered. What only Electron could add is looks: a borderless floating icon pinned to the corner like the Seewo toolbar, with no browser title bar. Electron over Tauri when we do: predictability and prior art matter more than footprint, since none of it can be tested from here.

## Deliberately not building

- **Reasons / labels on points** (ClassDojo-style). Teachers and admins report the taxonomy is the cumbersome part — it inserts a decision between the moment and the reward, which is fatal for something whose value is immediacy.
- **Further timer investment.** The smartboard has a timer, so do Gynzy and Classroom Screen. Ours is nice and costs nothing to keep, but nobody switches apps for a timer. **One exception, made on purpose: the dial** (Settled mechanics, "Timer"). It isn't a nicer clock: a child who can't yet read 4:53 can read a red that shrinks, and the teacher asked for it after using Gynzy's every day.
- **Whiteboard, lesson building, noise meter, AI images.** Gynzy's territory; we'd always be behind.
- **Student self-check-in for attendance.** Thirty kids tapping in creates a queue at the board; one teacher scanning the room takes ten seconds.
- **Fly-from-desks transition** for the flip deck. The slide is enough.

## Open questions

- **Are per-student point tallies actually wanted?** Derek's own practice is whole-class — marbles all land in one jar, no individual scorekeeping. The app is individual-first with the class meter riding along. That may be right for other teachers, or it may be the ClassDojo instinct sneaking in.
- Which existing features need tweaking. Derek reports several do; specifics need real classroom use to pin down.

## Settled mechanics

- **The app never scrolls.** Not the board, not a screen that slides over it, not the side panel. Everything is laid out to fit the viewport, and when there is more than fits, the layout adapts (fewer rows, smaller chips) rather than the page growing. A modal may scroll its *body*, but the modal itself always fits — it is capped at 90% of the viewport with a scrolling body, so its title and edges never leave the screen. This is a rule, not a preference: a teacher at a board never scrolls to find a control.

- **Smooth on the board: what moves, and how.** The teacher found the whole app laggy and choppy, at school and at home on any connection. It was never the internet: once the app is open nothing waits on it (each picture and sound is fetched the first time it's used). It was how hard the app made the device work, found by driving the production build at a board's speed - processor slowed 4x, 4K-like pixel density - and counting frames. Two causes. **Frosted glass**: the side panel, the goal bar, the flip-card and group toolbars, the dialog dim and the status picker blurred what was behind them, and a browser redoes every blur on every frame anything on screen moves. On a 4K board that halved the frame rate, and the dial timer, moving every frame inside the blurred panel, ran at 35. They are the same see-through colours without the blur; the dialog dim went from 40% to 50% and the status picker's to 60% so they still stand apart from the board. The only blur left is Delete Class's small safety cover, whose job is to hide the button under it. **Animations run by the main processor**: framer-motion moved things frame by frame in JavaScript - nine sparkles per point, stars swirling round a picked desk, thirty cards dealt, thirty name chips dealt, a spring on every button press, the flip clock's leaves every second - and made the page be measured on every render for a side-panel glide used once a term. **What went**, with the teacher asking for anything not really needed to go: the stars round a picked student or row (the picked desk now has a still amber outline, the pickers' colour everywhere else, and an inked black one in Comic Book, where amber disappears into the yellow ground), the +1 sparkles on the goal bar and, once it was live, the "+1" that rose off the coin on every point, and the one that rose off the floating window's +1 button (the count popping and the coin moving already say it), the brightness filter on a flickering desk or group card, and the side panel's glide when it changes sides. **What stayed, moved to CSS or `element.animate`**, which the graphics chip runs by itself, and looks the same: the goal bar's coin, fill, count pop and chest rattle; every button's press; the flip clock's leaves; the flip-card deal, turn, lift and fade; and the group deal, where each chip remembers where it was drawn and glides home from there - one small glide for gathering, dealing, moving a name and the chips closing up behind it. The dial redraws only as often as its edge moves, every half degree (about 12 times a second for a 1-minute timer, once a second for 20 minutes), not 60 times a second. And the windows no longer build their contents while closed: Class Settings built the roster and a month of attendance on every desk tap. Measured at 4K-like density with the processor slowed 4x, frames per second before → after: dial timer 35 → 57, flipping cards 30 → 52, +1 39 → 56, picking a student 46 → 58, dealing groups 33 → 50, opening flip cards 38 → 50, tapping desks 50 → 56. What is left is a one-off pause as the flip-card and group screens are first built. The rules this leaves are in CLAUDE.md; `scripts/frame-check.mjs` repeats the measurement.

- **The side panel grows with a tall screen.** Its text is sized in vmin, so on a 1080-tall board the text grew while the panel stayed 16rem: the class name was cut to "4B Eng…" and Swap Seats ran out past the panel's edge. The panel is now 30vmin, held between 16rem and 21rem. That is unchanged up to about 850px tall, which is where the text starts growing, and in step with it from there to where the text stops, so what fits at 1280x800 fits on a bigger board. The desks at 1920x1080 are a little narrower for it.
- **The class name has the top row to itself**, with only the class switcher's arrow and a **gear for Class Settings** beside it. Attendance used to share that row and left the name about 100px, or 67px with the arrow, so "Grade 4 English" read "Grade …". Every idea that gave the name its own row pushed the whole panel down, so the top two rows swapped contents instead and nothing moved: Class Settings became a gear beside the class name, the way the pickers' settings are a gear beside theirs, and **Attendance went beside Swap Seats**, where the word Settings was. Those two are the desk modes - each turns a desk tap into something else and only one can be on - so the one that's on lights up next to the one greyed out. The name now gets about 160px at 1280x800 with two classes. Checked in the real faces (Andika, Cabin Sketch, Bangers): "Grade 4 English" is full size in every theme at 1024x640, 1280x800 and 1920x1080, where before it was cut with two classes in all of them, and the Pickers & Points box sits 1-5px higher (2px lower in Comic Book at 1920, whose name is now full size). Settings lost its word; the gear says Class Settings on hover, and only the teacher uses it. **A name that still doesn't fit shrinks, by up to a quarter, before it truncates**: "Kindergarten Phonics" is at 83-98% depending on the theme, and uncut. It is measured in the page rather than with `fitText`, because Chalkboard and Comic Book letter the class name in faces wider than Andika.

- **Desks: thirty, and a seventh column for a class of more than thirty (35 at most).** Six across and five deep fits most classes and looks right, so it stays exactly as it is; the seventh column appears on the right only when the roster passes thirty (or someone is still sitting in it), with no setting to find. The new desks are numbered 30-34 on the end of the seating list rather than re-numbering the grid seven wide, because re-numbering would have scrambled every saved seating chart; saves from before are padded with five empty desks on load. A 30-desk board was checked pixel-for-pixel against the old one. In a big class every desk is a seventh narrower and the names shrink with it (they are sized to the desk). Seat Class fills the seven columns bottom row first like the six; Pick Row picks from seven rows (only ever one with somebody in it today - a class that doesn't fill the grid, or a row whose students are all away, could land on a row of nobody); Split by Rows can make seven groups; flip cards lay out seven wide past thirty (35 is exactly five full rows). **Known tight spot:** Pairs with 35 students makes 17 groups, which at 1024x640 squeezes the cards to six narrow columns, small names and a cut-off "Group…" title. Fine at 1280x800 and up.
- **Attendance: one button, one mark, nothing else.** An informal record for the teacher, not a register for the school, so it is kept as small as it can be. **Attendance** sits beside Swap Seats, the other desk mode (it was on the class's own row until that row was needed for long class names). Tapping it turns the mode on, and then the only thing that answers is a desk: tap marks a student absent, tap again brings them back. Everything else on the panel stands down, as it does for Swap Seats, and the button stands down itself while flip cards or group cards cover the desks. Turning it off records the day, so a class with nobody away gets its check from on-then-off, and the button carries a green check for the rest of the day. The label never changes. **Absent is the only mark**: Late, Excused, a "Here now" pop-up for latecomers, a Done button and an "Everyone's here" button were all planned and dropped as clutter; a student who turns up late is just tapped back in with the mode on. An absent desk shows a **zzz** in place of the avatar and fades a little, the name still readable. One picture for everyone, because only 7 of the 20 sticker packs have a sleepy pose; the zzz was chosen over a thinner "dream cloud" because thick strokes survive a projector. **Absent lasts the day it was marked** and no longer: the record is keyed by local date, so tomorrow starts with everyone present (a date changing, not a timer). A student marked away ahead of time (below) simply starts that day away. For the rest of that day an absent student counts as not seated for everything that chooses students: Pick Student and Pick Row skip them, the flip deck doesn't deal them, Pick All and Everyone +1 leave them out, tapping their desk doesn't select them, and new groups and the Group Activity captions count only who is here. In "Continue with Last Groups" they stay on their team's card, faded with a small zzz, since the team is whole again tomorrow, but the group pickers pass them by and they get no stars when the group's points go out. **The record** is a month grid in Class Settings → Students → View / Edit Attendance, on the roster's header, and an Export to CSV. No print button: the browser can print. **It can be edited**: tap a square to mark a student away or bring them back, on any day - fixing a past mistake, or marking a trip next week. So every Monday to Friday gets a column, not only days attendance was taken (a missed day or a day ahead needs somewhere to tap); taken days read at full strength, the rest are quieter, today is highlighted, the weeks are ruled off, and the month arrows go forward without end. A square ignores a second tap for 0.7 s, because the board reads one touch as two. **Editing the record is not taking attendance**: a student marked away for Friday doesn't give Friday its green check, or one early absence would look like the whole class had been checked. Anything done in attendance mode at the board is taking it. The export has a column for every day that was taken or has someone marked away. The record lives in `ClassData.attendance` as date → absent ids, and `attendanceTaken` lists the days actually taken; saves from before that list read every recorded day as taken, which is what they meant, and any change writes the list out. Back up / Restore of all data was discussed and deferred.
- **Points**: floored at 0. Select students (tap desks), then +/− in the side panel. Selection is keyed by student id, which is what lets desks and flip cards share the same controls. **− greys out when nobody selected has a star to lose** ("No points to take away" on hover), the way a group card's − already did at zero: it used to play the point-lost sound over a student who had nothing to lose, which the class hears as a telling-off for nothing. With several selected it stays live while any of them has a star.
- **Picker sounds: a soft pop on every other flash, then a gentle chime** - for every picker (Pick Student, Pick Row, the group pickers and the floating class goal). The teacher chose the shape by ear on a listening page ("Floating Pick Sounds", a Claude artifact) that played a whole pick three ways: the old sound, a dry 3.5kHz tick on every flash at full volume with a pop-and-sparkle landing, was "too jarring" and he wanted it out of the app altogether; the soft pop at half volume on every flash was good but "still a lot of pops"; the same pop (pop.mp3) on every other flash, with a soft ding to land, was the one he liked. The desk or name still changes every 90ms; only the sound is halved. **Then it was tuned on the board**, in a standalone preview of the app with an example class (a Claude artifact, "Softer Picker Preview"): at a third of full volume the pops needed the speakers all the way up, and the single 1.3kHz ding, which started at full strength, came out "loud and abrupt" after them. Measured offline, the quiet pops and the ding were about equally loud, and 1.3kHz is where classroom speakers and ears are strongest while 500Hz is near the bottom of what the speakers do. So the pops went up to 80% (8 dB louder) and the ding became a chime: G, C and E rolled upward 70ms apart, each fading in over 40ms and ringing about a second, with a faint octave for shimmer - its loudest 50ms about 2 dB under the ding's. Heard again in the preview on the board: "MUCH better", and it went live. tick.mp3 was deleted.
- **Point sound: an arcade coin, one sound for every point** - the class goal meter moving up (points from the desks, +1 in the floating window, Everyone +1) and +1 on a group card, which used to have a ping of its own. The old pings (two quick sine tones, the meter's topping out near 2kHz, the sharpest spot on a board speaker) were jarring, and the teacher asked for a coin "like in Mario, or some sort of chime". A listening page ("Point Award Sounds", a Claude artifact) played four at matched loudness (within 0.3 dB of the old ping), one point or a quick run of three: the old ping, a coin, a small bell, and the single marble from the Marble Sound Lab tuned lower. He chose the coin: "great". It is a short G then a C that rings about half a second, in a triangle wave with a faint octave on top - a coin in spirit, not Nintendo's own sound, and rounder and lower than a console's buzzy square wave. The marble lost to it for points; the Lab's other marble ideas (the pour, the swirl) stay parked.
- **Minus sound: "Negative reverberate", one low note that drops and fades, at 43% volume.** The recording before it was the teacher's own earlier pick, but it played at full volume, about 14 dB louder than the coin (measured offline, loudest 50ms: -8.9 dB against -22.7), and the room heard it as a blast. A listening page ("Minus Point Sounds", a Claude artifact) offered it turned down, the coin played backwards, a soft buzzer and a wooden plonk, and he liked none of them. He then sent nine recordings of his own. They were measured rather than heard: a low beep (99% under 250Hz) and a bongo were ruled out as too low for a board speaker, a punch because a hitting sound for a child losing a point is the wrong picture, and two pops because the picker already pops. Of three trimmed and levelled on a second page ("Your Minus Sounds"), he chose Negative reverberate. **It breaks the keep-above-400Hz rule on purpose**: nearly all of it is 280-400Hz, and it was picked by ear anyway. If it goes quiet on the board, its volume is the first knob. It is levelled by A-weighting rather than plain loudness, because the ear hears a low note as quieter: about 1.3 dB under the coin to the ear. The file's 71ms of silence at the front is skipped so it lands with the tap, and the synthesised fallback (for when the recording won't play) was turned down to match; at full it was the old blast again. **Listening-page trap**: both pages first played none of the recordings, because the artifact viewer blocks `fetch()`, even of a `data:` address. Recordings on a listening page are decoded from base64 with `atob`, never fetched.
- **Timer: a flip clock or a dial.** The dial is the Time Timer the teacher uses in Gynzy every day: a red that is the time left, shrinking back towards the top with the hand on its edge. **Timer Settings picks between them with two small pictures**, Flip Clock and Dial, rather than a switch, because a switch reads as on and off and this is one timer or the other. Both share the controls (minutes, seconds, start, pause, stop) and the six alarm sounds. **The controls open from a tap on the timer itself**, and a second tap shuts them: the words "Timer controls" under both timers were taken out so the clock gets the room. It is hidden on purpose, for the teacher's tutorial to show. A second tap within 0.7 s is ignored, because the board reads one touch as two and the second would shut what the first opened; taps inside the open controls don't count, and the timer ignores taps while Swap Seats or Attendance is on. **The red always starts as a full circle for the time set**, where Gynzy's face is a fixed 60 minutes: at side-panel size a 3-minute timer on a 60-minute face is a sliver that barely moves. Full is all the time left, half is half gone. No numbers round the edge, too small to read from the back, so twelve ticks instead, and the time in numbers beside the dial for the teacher. **It is side-panel size**, not the big dial Gynzy draws, and within that **the dial is the point**: it is what the children read, so it takes about 62% of the box's width (124px across at 1280x800, 167px at 1920x1080) and the time sits small beside it for the teacher, centred top to bottom. The first version made the time as big as the flip digits and the dial only 85px, and the teacher asked for the other way round. The dial is capped at 15.5% of the screen's height, so on a short screen it gives way before the panel runs out: at 1024x640 with the controls open the panel is 38px short with the dial, the size of the exception already accepted, and the flip clock now fits. A big view over the board is the next step if the dial proves too small in class. It redraws every frame while running, so the red shrinks smoothly rather than a step a second. **Time's up** (the teacher's own addition): "Time's up!" on a pale red disc where the red was, breathing slowly, until the teacher taps Stop or sets a new time; nothing ends it on a clock. Gentle, because the class goal is the one big moment. Stop now stays live once the time has run out, since it's what clears the words; it used to grey out at 00:00. **Left out of Gynzy's settings**: the direction (only the one the teacher uses), the colour (red only, the same in every theme, like the group colours), and the 25% warning applies only to the flip clock, because the shrinking red already shows time running out.
- **Flip Cards: one tap, one meaning, nothing timed.** A student comes up and taps a card; whoever it reveals has the turn, and the side panel's +/- go to them - no selecting a card first, because a tap on a face-up card is how it gets put away. The newest card glows amber; earlier face-up cards dim but stay readable, and stay up. Tapping a faded card gives it the turn back (a spelling bee's "try again") and fades whoever had it. **Only the glowing card can be put away**, and what that tap does is the one mode, **Flip Back** or **Discard**, and it sits on the flip toolbar rather than in settings because it changes what the tap means. Timed flip-back and set-aside were built and taken out: the teacher, not a clock, decides when a turn ends. So discarding a faded card takes two deliberate taps - wake it, then put it away - which is the protection a double-tap-to-discard would have given, without asking for a gesture the smart board fakes on its own. Putting away the active card leaves nobody active and +/- greyed rather than quietly handing the points back to someone who already had their turn. Reveal All is nobody's turn (every card at full strength, +/- greyed); Hide All only turns cards over - only a tap reaches the discard pile; Shuffle brings everything back. When the cards don't fill the last row, the short row goes on top: the bottom rows are the ones a small child can reach, so those are the ones kept full. The short row starts at the left, like a line of writing, so the empty spaces sit together at the top right (28 cards on six columns leaves the last two top-right slots empty). It was centred at first, which left a hole on each side and looked like cards were missing. The deal matches: cards land bottom row first, left to right, working up, so the short row is the last to arrive. Every card keeps its slot for the whole round: a discard leaves an empty space rather than letting the rest close up, so no card grows, shrinks or moves while the class is choosing. A discarded card flies over the board onto the pile, and the pile counts it when it lands. **Bonus cards** (off by default, a switch in flip card settings): Everyone +1, Jackpot +3 and Oops!. Pick a Friend was proposed and dropped - picking a friend is what the student at the board is already doing. Face down they look exactly like student cards, borrowing a random student's back colour, so nobody can hunt for them. The app picks how many (3-6, fewer for a small class) and the column count together so the board comes out as full rows; of the class sizes the chart can hold, only 7 and 10 can't. Everyone +1 lands the way Pick All then + would, class meter included; Jackpot waits on the toolbar ("Next card +3") and lands on the next student turned face up, stacking if two come first; Oops! does nothing. The only "bad" card is a dud because a flip is luck, and losing stars to luck isn't fair to the student who earned them. A bonus card is never anybody's turn: the glow stays where it was, and one tap puts a face-up bonus card away - **onto the discard pile, in Flip Back mode too**. Turned back over, it paid out again every time it was found, and a class soon remembers where Everyone +1 is. Only a hand-flip pays out - Reveal All never does. Reveals are small (an icon pop or wobble, a short sparkle or wah-wah): the class goal is the one big celebration in the app, and a fanfare for every lucky card would wear it down. Turning bonus cards on or changing the kinds re-deals straight away if nobody has touched the deal yet; mid-round it waits for Shuffle. The side panel names the student ("Points go to Mulan") only on the flip cards, on one line that is always there so nothing jumps, and Pick All stands down so +/- never mean the whole room. Pick Student and Pick Row stand down too: the flip cards *are* the picker, and a pick behind the deck lit up desks nobody could see. Opening the cards clears a finished pick, and Flip Cards waits while a pick is still flashing. A card ignores taps for 0.7 s after it turns: smart boards read one touch as two, and in Discard mode that second tap would throw away the card it just revealed.
- **Class goal meter**: one shared total. Deductions affect a student's own tally but never pull the class meter backward — it's collective momentum, not a ledger. On reaching the goal: celebrate, then reset to 0 for the next goal. **The celebration keys off a "goals reached" counter, never off the total falling** — a reset and a correction are falls too, and Reset Class Goal used to throw the party (with whichever gif had last been decoded, which could be one the teacher had switched away from). **The meter can be corrected** with a "class points on the meter now" stepper in Pickers & Points, beside the goal stepper: a point that landed by mistake had no way back short of resetting the whole run. It's held to the goal and never opens the chest. **With the goal switched off, stars don't move the meter**: it used to fill out of sight, so switching the goal back on showed progress the class never watched, and a goal could pass with no party. **A goal lowered below what's on the meter leaves it full** (it read "40 / 30"), and the next point opens the chest. **Points past the goal carry into the next run and show once the chest shuts**: the bar used to sit at 0 over them until the next point. Points given while the chest is open wait for it to shut, and a second goal filled mid-party doesn't start a second fanfare on top.
- **Floating class goal: the meter and one +1, over the lesson.** Derek watched a colleague lose lesson time switching tabs to ClassDojo and back, and the school's Seewo toolbar showed the shape of the answer: a small tool that floats over whatever is on the board. Because this app's points build to a whole-class goal, the floating version can be one button - a marble in the jar - where Dojo's would need the class list, since Dojo can't give a point without finding the child. That is why the parked version (a scrolling name list with +/- beside each name) was not what got built. **Float** sits on the meter itself, at its right end, because the meter is what floats; it costs the side panel nothing and only exists when there is a goal. It stays lit while floating and tapping it again brings the goal back; the window's own close and back-to-tab buttons close it too, and switching the goal off takes it away. The window holds the class name, the fill, the chest and the count, and a +1 that adds a class point straight to the goal (`addToClassGoal`), with nobody's stars behind it. It follows whichever class is open. +1 ignores a second tap for 0.7 s, like the flip cards and the attendance squares, because the board reads one touch as two. **A goal filled from the float doesn't celebrate behind the lesson.** The celebration is the one big moment and it needs the full screen, so the chest waits, full and rattling, while the app isn't in front (not showing, or not the window last tapped), and the float's +1 becomes **Celebrate!**. Tapping it asks the browser to bring the app forward; the chest opens the moment the app is in front, however it got there. If the browser won't bring it forward, the button adds "Open the app to see it". Nothing plays from behind the lesson, and filling the goal in the app itself celebrates exactly as before. **How**: Chrome and Edge's Document Picture-in-Picture, an always-on-top window the page opens on a tap and draws into through a portal, so it is the same app and the same save - a +1 there moves the meter here instantly. It copies the page's styles and follows the theme. Firefox and Safari don't have it, so the button isn't shown there. Everything that moves in it is CSS: the page that draws it gets no animation frames while the lesson covers it, so framer-motion would freeze halfway. It opens at 340x180 and can be moved and resized. **Its limits**: the browser gives it a thin title bar with the site's address and its own back-to-tab and close buttons, so it can never be a bare floating icon like Seewo's - only the desktop program (Electron, parked) could do that. Testing note: Playwright stretches the floating window to the test's viewport, and any request route stops the window loading its pictures, so tests set its size back and pass fonts in as a style tag. **Pick Student in the float** (added once the float had proved itself on the board): **Pick** sits beside +1, which stays the big button. It is the app's own Pick Student - same pace, same sounds, same Allow Repeats and pick history, and it skips anyone away today - so the desks behind light up too, and a pick started from the side panel shows in the float as well. The desks can't be seen, so the float shows the pick as a name as big as the window allows: flickering past, then landed and ringed in the picker's amber. The name stays until it is tapped (nothing timed), and that tap also clears the pick in the app. +1 always means the class goal, even with a name up: giving the picked student a star from here would give one button two meanings. Pick stands down whenever the side panel's would (Flip Cards, Group Activity, Swap Seats, Attendance, a pick already running). The 0.7 s tap guard is shared by every button in the float, because clearing a name puts +1 and Pick back under the same finger. **The flicker runs on the floating window's clock**: the app's page is behind the lesson, and Chrome slows a hidden page's timers to about one a second, so the pick is paced by the float's own timer (`pickStudent(clock)`). If the float closes mid-flash that clock stops with it, so the pick is cancelled rather than left flashing forever. **It shrinks to a strip in one tap**, because Derek wanted it see-through so it wouldn't block the lesson, and a web page can't make its window see-through. A shrink button at the end of the meter row turns the window into one row, 240x62 (the smallest Chrome will resize it to; 240x52 only when opening): the count, +1 and Pick, and a grow button that puts it back at whatever size it was before. In the strip the class name, fill and chest give way, Pick loses its icon, a pick is a name the height of the strip, and Celebrate reads "Open the app" once tapped. The layout follows the window's own height (under 120px is the strip), so dragging an edge small gives the strip too. The resize is `resizeTo` from the tap, measured against the window's title bar and edges, which differ between computers; the window keeps its top-left corner where it was. No button back to the full app yet: the window's title bar has the browser's own. Flip Cards, Group Activity and Settings stay in the app: they need the whole board. The clock stays out: the board's own toolbar has one.
- **Two knobs on the goal, not one.** Stars convert to class points at a teacher-set rate, and the goal is counted in class points. A divisor doesn't change *pacing* — the goal alone already does, since "10 stars each, goal 8" is arithmetically "goal 80" — it changes the number the class looks at. 5/8 is a jar filling; 48/80 is a score. Leftover stars are banked, so awarding one at a time eventually counts for as much as awarding them all at once. Default is 1:1, so classes saved before this behave exactly as they did.
- **Resetting stars and resetting the meter are separate actions.** They were briefly one, on the grounds that both describe the same term — but that argues for making both easy, not for forcing a teacher who wants one to destroy the other.
- **Class Settings is two tabs, Students and Class.** It was one page grouped by row rather than meaning, and the roster - the part used most - got a row and a half of it on a big class. **Students** (opens first from the side panel): Add a Student, then the roster, which takes all the height left, with Import CSV then **View / Edit Attendance** on its header (a CSV can still be dropped on the roster; the big drop box went, since on a board it was tapped anyway). **Importing skips anyone already on the roster** (same name and homeroom), so bringing in a file a second time, after fixing a line in it, adds only who is new rather than doubling the class; the same file can be chosen twice in a row, which used to do nothing at all. A roster edit that wipes out a name keeps the old one, and deleting a student takes them out of the last groups and the attendance record too. **There is no roster export**: the teacher saw no need for it, so the button and its code went. The foot runs in the order a class is set up: **Student Avatars** with a one-line note of what it does, then Unseat All beside Seat Students, because they are opposites. Sharing the foot row costs the roster no height, which a row of its own would have (about one student fewer at 1024x640). The attendance button is named for what it opens - the record - because attendance is *taken* on the seating chart with the side panel's button, not here. **The gender box reads "Boy or Girl" until one is picked**, in the placeholder's grey: it has no label of its own and "Unspecified" didn't say what it was for. Boy and Girl are the only choices; a student can't be set back to not chosen, though a CSV without a gender column still brings them in that way. **Class**: New Class and Delete Class together at the top (the delete cover's note has room there; it used to run into New Class at 1920 wide), then the class name and the theme (marked as changing every class, since it does). **A class made from the splash opens on this tab** (Create My First Class and New Class alike), because the first thing a new class needs is its name. The class name belongs to the class, not the students, which is why it moved here from beside Attendance. **Themes are picked from small drawings** - a strip of side panel and six desks - in each theme's real colours: the theme tokens also apply under `[data-theme-preview]`, so a change to a theme shows in its thumbnail by itself, plus a mark or two per theme (Comic Book's ink and dots, Chalkboard's wooden frame). **Student Avatars moved to the Students tab**, because it acts on every student, and it is named that rather than Class Avatars for the same reason. A strip of the class's first ten avatars beside it was taken out: a short note saying what the button does is enough. What space is left on the Class tab stays empty rather than being filled for the sake of it. The window holds one height so switching tabs doesn't resize it.
- **Points settings live in Pickers & Points**, not Class Settings, with per-student editing in the roster (the only per-student surface). Class Settings is for the class's composition and appearance.
- **Flip-card picker**: slides over the desk grid rather than taking the full screen, so the side panel's point buttons and the goal meter stay live. Seated students only. Its own round, independent of Pick Student's history — setting a card aside *is* the no-repeat mechanism. Gender colouring on card backs is cosmetic (all students dealt), and flipping awards no points automatically.
- **Avatars**: Flaticon sticker packs (paid subscription), imported by `import-stickers.py` into `public/avatars/stickers/<theme>/<pose>.svg`. This replaced the Google Flow set and fixed the proportion drift outright — every sticker is drawn on the same 512×512 canvas, so nothing needs per-image CSS. A student with no avatar gets a character derived from their id, so a freshly imported class already looks varied. **Every pose on offer is one a teacher is happy to see on a desk.** The teacher went through all 468 stickers on a review page and took out 138: crying, sad, angry, sick, hurt and scary ones, plus poses that read as grumpy, shocked, or odd for a classroom (skulls, swords, a beer, piles of money). They are listed in `stickers/removed.txt` rather than deleted, so the paid packs stay whole and re-running the import never brings them back. Lion, Ghost and Wolf lost their automatic resting face (the angry wolf was every unpicked wolf's face), so the import names a smiling one for each. A student who was wearing a removed pose keeps their character on its resting face rather than turning into another animal.
- **Bulk avatar assignment**: Class Settings → Class → Student Avatars. Scope (everyone / boys / girls) × poses (mixed / same for all), then one tap applies; tapping the same character again re-rolls. Immediate rather than a confirm step — the undo is tapping something else.
- **Typeface**: Andika, app-wide. Chosen for its single-storey lowercase `a`, which is how the students are taught to write it; Comic Sans was rejected. Lexend was a close second. `lib/fitText.ts` measures in Andika because it picks one name size for a whole class — measuring in the wrong face would size every desk wrong.
- **Desk layout**: rounded top, square bottom. Homeroom number top-left, points badge top-right, both over the avatar's empty corners, leaving the whole bottom row to the name. One name size per class, computed from the longest name, so no student's desk reads smaller than the rest. **Names use their own colour, `--desk-name`, softer than the theme's text colour.** On a smartboard the names were the loudest thing on screen — big, bold and near-black at about 14:1 contrast, against 6:1 for the app's own button text — and read as glaring. Size was not the problem: a smaller name was still harsh, and a lighter weight is a big jump because Andika has only regular and bold, and thin strokes are the first thing a projector washes out. So the names stay bold and full size, and Light and Elementary each use a dark tinted from their own palette at about 9:1, well clear of the 7:1 readability bar. Dark (white on dark) and Comic Book (ink on purpose) keep their card text colour, and Chalkboard writes them in chalk white on a desk a shade deeper than the board, also about 9:1.
- **Themes**: five, chosen in Class Settings → Class — Light, Dark, Chalkboard, Elementary (the default until a teacher picks otherwise; only a pick in the picker is remembered, never the OS preference) and Comic Book. School Colors, built from the Yuteh crest, was removed at the teacher's request. Sky Blue made way for Chalkboard, in its slot in the picker: Light, Sky Blue and Elementary were all light and blue, so it was the one most like the others. A teacher who had a removed theme picked lands on Elementary. Three of the five are pure CSS-variable swaps and can stay that way. **Comic Book is the exception and is allowed to be**: what makes a page look printed is ink weight, a halftone lattice and hard shadows, none of which is a colour. So it carries a structural block in `index.css` alongside its tokens, under two rules that keep that from spreading. It only reaches for hooks that already exist — `data-slot` on the shadcn primitives, plus `data-ink` on the three app containers that had none (canvas, panel, desk) — and it never touches anything that affects layout: every outline is a `box-shadow` ring rather than a border, so switching themes cannot reflow the board. Bangers letters the chrome; student names stay in Andika, because an all-caps display face is the wrong thing for a roster of real names read across a room. Colour varies per desk through a rotating five, carried in a caption bar across the panel's top rather than a wash or a centred glow — both of those put saturated colour behind the student's name. Texture alternates between a halftone lattice and diagonal speed ruling, so neighbouring panels aren't printed the same way. **Chalkboard is the second exception, under the same two rules.** It came from the teacher's picture of a green classroom board with coloured chalk doodles and a wooden ledge. The board is chalkboard green with chalk dust (the splash screen's grain) and a wooden frame, drawn as an inset shadow inside the canvas's own padding so nothing moves. Desks are slate tiles a shade deeper than the board, outlined in soft white chalk: the side panel buttons' chalk, drawn a little heavier, with the homeroom number in the same chalk a step quieter than the name. The first version gave each desk one of five chalk colours on a diagonal, like Comic Book's caption bars, and on a real board thirty coloured outlines were a distraction from the names. It is a dark theme underneath, so the `dark:` variant covers it as well as Dark. The splash screen's chalk face (Cabin Sketch) letters the class name, panel headings and dialog titles; names stay in Andika for the same reason as Comic Book. Switched-on buttons are yellow chalk, dialogs get the wooden frame, and the flip deck and group cards get the dust through `data-ink="board"`. Flip card backs and group colours stay as they are in every theme.

- **Group Activity**: one button on the side panel, one modal, one screen. The modal is all buttons, no typing: how many groups (2–6), groups of (pairs, 3s, 4s, 5s), or split by boys/girls or rows, each captioned with what it makes of *this* class today ("7–8 each", "15 groups") and greyed out with the reason when it can't. Tapping one deals: the chips gather into a stack in the middle of the board and are dealt out one at a time into colour-banded cards, with the side panel's pickers and +/- standing down the way they do for Swap Seats — the timer stays live. Building groups by hand is deliberately not a mode (thirty students is sixty taps); fix-ups are tap a chip, then tap the card it belongs in — anywhere on the card, chips included. Dragging was built and taken out: on a touch board it fights with scrolling, and one way to do a thing is easier to teach than two. Chips are name and homeroom number only, no avatars, **one size for the whole class regardless of how many groups there are**, all the same width (set by the longest name, like the desks' one name size), so they line up in rows; a name past the cap shrinks inside its chip. Cards fit their contents and every card in a deal shares the tallest one's height, centred on the board, so empty space is around the cards rather than inside them. **The board never scrolls**: a planner knows every card's dimensions, so it picks the column count that fits at full-size chips, then tightens the card's chrome (three densities: padding, light size and button size give way in that order), and only when nothing fits does it shrink the chips a step (all of them, so one size for the class holds) and try again — names are what has to be read from the back of the room, so they are the last thing to give. The chrome is computed from the classes, not measured, since the plan decides which density renders; the frame is a real border, since a ring outside the card was clipped wherever it met the board's edge. Leftovers spread into existing groups rather than forming a group of one. Each card carries its own score, with the same +/- as the side panel. **Exit Group Activity** is the one way out: with nothing scored it just closes; with points on the board it asks whether they go out now or wait with the groups — handing thirty students their stars is not a side effect of leaving a screen. Points go where the toggle at the bottom of the modal says, remembered per class: a star per point to every member, or one class point per group point onto the goal meter. Choosing Class Goal with no goal switches the class goal on right there (greying the button out sent the teacher looking for a bug). Groups survive exit, so "Continue with Last Groups" picks the same teams up tomorrow, scores included if they were kept; anyone no longer seated drops out on the way; a shuffle keeps each card's identity by position so only the chips move. Colours are fixed across themes (they *are* the group's identity); everything else follows the theme, like Flip Cards. Names are Group 1, 2, 3 (or Boys and Girls) and can't be changed, so nothing in Group Activity asks anyone to type.
  - **Status.** Groups are for projects as much as games, so every card carries one of four states: Working, Help, Ready, Done. Ready and Done are deliberately different — Ready means a group has stopped and is waiting for the teacher, Done means there is nothing left to do. **The status colours the card, it is not a widget on the card.** The body and the points row both take a **strong** wash in the status colour and the band and frame keep the group's own colour, so scanning the room is "look for the red card", which is the red, amber and green cups on a group's table. The wash is deliberately heavy: the first attempt used a 16% tint and from the back of a room it was indistinguishable from the next card. Name chips sit on the plain card colour so they stay crisp on top of it. The small status chip in the band wears the status colour too, ringed in the band's own text colour so it still separates when a red group is the one asking for help — a faded chip said nothing at a distance and the chip and the body should say the same thing. Working has no wash at all: if every card were tinted the board would go one flat colour and a single red card would stop standing out. Help pulses a soft ring, and it is the only status that moves, because it is the only one asking for you now. Icons are Pencil, question mark, thumbs up and tick, chosen to be read without English — a raised hand turns to mush at small sizes and a flag has to be taught. Help, Ready and Done chime, switchable in the Group Activity modal. Statuses survive Exit and Continue and reset with a new deal.
  - **Group names cannot be edited.** They are Group 1, 2, 3 or Boys and Girls, and that is enough — a teacher who wants "Red Team" can say it. Renaming existed briefly and its field stretched from the name to the status chip, which mostly served as a way for a student to open a dialog by accident. Removing it removed the accident and a whole modal.
    - **The first version was a strip of four lights on the card and it was wrong twice over.** The lit light expanded in place, so its position moved from card to card and a board of fifteen never lined up; and the strip had a fixed 200px minimum the layout planner never reserved, so at fifteen groups it was clipped at both ends. Cards were optimised one at a time and never looked at fifteen side by side.
  - **Choosing a status is a panel low on the screen, not buttons on the card.** A card in the top row of a smartboard is two metres up and a small card would mean small buttons, which is worst for the shortest student. So the board dims, the card flies down to a fixed spot and four large buttons sit beneath it in the bottom third. One row, left to right, in the order work goes: a two by two grid leaves a child guessing whether to read across or down. Every button wears its status colour, so the code is taught each time. **No close button and no instructions**: the group's current status is one of the four and is shown as already chosen, so a child who opened it by accident taps the lit one and nothing happens. Tapping outside closes it too. The panel is solid over a board dimmed by 60% rather than frosted — the deep dim is what makes it feel lifted, and two translucent layers read as muddy on a projector that has already eaten the contrast. It was a blur, which a 4K board redid on every frame anything moved behind it (see Smooth on the board).
  - **The points row is sized by the card's width, not by the density.** Density is chosen to make cards fit by *height*, so a board that went to eight columns could leave a card half as wide carrying a row built for a wide one — which is how the plus and minus ended up clipped at the card's edges. Widths and icon sizes now come from the card's width while heights still come from the density, so the planner's arithmetic stays true.
  - **Picking, during a group activity, reuses the side panel's two picker buttons.** Pick Row becomes **Pick Group** while the cards are up — a row and a group are both "a set of students", so the button keeps its meaning and only what counts as a set changes — and Pick Student flashes across the name chips instead of the desks. The pickers are a family and a teacher already looks in that box for them; putting one in the group toolbar would split them across two places and add a sixth control to a row that wraps. They were greyed out before because the desk picker lights up desks, which are behind the cards and so invisible here, so `useGroupPicker` is a separate hook at the same pace and with the same sounds, pointed at the cards. The winner is ringed in amber, the one colour that is never a group's and never a status. It honours the same Allow Repeats setting, tracked per activity and reset on a new deal, and a landed pick clears on a tap anywhere.
    - Two framer-motion traps here, both worth remembering: it will not clear an inline `box-shadow` it has set, so the winner's ring had to be a class or every past winner stayed ringed; and on an element with a `layoutId` the shared-layout pass writes an inline `opacity` that no utility class can beat, so a dimmed chip's opacity has to go through `animate`. (The chips no longer use framer at all - see Smooth on the board - but the traps stand for anything that still does.)
  - **The riffle belongs to the Shuffle button.** A first deal played it too, so making groups sounded like two sounds landing on top of each other; there is nothing to shuffle when the cards are being made for the first time.
  - **Reset Points** sits in the group toolbar, greyed until something has been scored, behind a confirm. It zeroes the scores and leaves the groups alone, for running the same teams through a second round.
  - **Settings and Swap Seats stand down during a group activity.** Both rearrange the class underneath a running activity, and Swap Seats acts on desks that are behind the cards. Swap Seats is out whenever the cards are up; Settings goes out when the board is locked, which is when students are the ones standing at it.
  - **Lock** is for students coming up to the board. It freezes what would destroy work: moving a name, New Groups, Shuffle, Exit and the side panel's button. **Points and status stay live**, because awarding a point for being on task mid activity is normal and unlocking to do it would be backwards. It is a courtesy lock, not a PIN.

- **Deployment**: pushes to `main` auto-deploy to https://a102032.github.io/seating/. The workflow curls the published URL afterwards and fails if it isn't serving the built bundle — Pages served the repo's raw `index.html` for several runs while every step reported green. `build-artifact.py` bundles a standalone copy for the Claude artifact preview.

## Known loose ends

- **Numbered sticker packs have no pose names.** 13 of the 20 packs are numbered (`owl-002`), so `absent` can't be detected for them. Their glum poses were taken out by eye (see Avatars), so bulk assignment no longer needs names to avoid them.
- **Andika ships only weights 400 and 700.** `font-semibold` renders identically to `font-bold` throughout the app. Nothing looks broken, but the type has one less step than the design assumed.
