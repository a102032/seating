# Decisions

Running record of what we've settled and why, so the reasoning survives between sessions.
Newest thinking at the top of each section. Last updated 2026-10-02. Loaded into every new Claude Code session through CLAUDE.md.

## What this app is

- **A seating chart, attendance and points tool that runs *alongside* Gynzy / NaniBox / Classroom Screen** — not a replacement for them. Gynzy already does whiteboard, lessons, timers and noise meters well; its seating chart and attendance are weak. That gap is the product.
- **Target user: teachers who want marble-jar dynamics but can't have the jar.** Derek's room runs on a physical marble jar, a per-class jar, and a battery wall that fills toward a treasure chest. Colleagues like watching it work and default to ClassDojo instead because they can't replicate it.
- **Deliberately fewer features than ClassDojo**: no parent accounts, no behaviour taxonomy, no setup. The things people complain about are the things we leave out. That's a position, not a limitation.
- **The name is Class? Yes!**, the call-and-response every teacher already uses to get the room: the teacher calls, the whole class answers. It is a whole-class moment, which is what the app is for. Written ClassYes wherever punctuation can't go (web address, app stores, search); classyes.app is the address to buy, and classyes.com is a premium domain worth buying only once teachers are paying. Names with "jar" were ruled out because ClassJar, Jar of Stars and Classroom Screen's behaviour jar already exist; Class Go, Class Ready, Ready Class and Ready to Learn all clash with products or programmes that exist. The phrase is too common to trademark on its own.
- **The logo is drawn in code, not a picture.** Two chalk speech bubbles, "Class?" in white and "Yes!" answering in yellow (`ClassYesLogo.tsx`), redrawn from a Gemini sketch. As an SVG it stays sharp on any board, and the words are live Andika, so the `a` is the one the students write. It is the splash screen's headline, with "Welcome, Teacher!" moved under it as a smaller line. The 2+2=4 doodle moved left to make room, because the taller headline pushed the button down on top of it. It moved again when Sign in with Google widened the row under the classes: it is up the left edge now, between the sun and the cat, and the chalk star sits between the dog and the house. **A splash taller than the screen is scaled down as a whole to fit**, rather than squeezed: five classes with Sign in on a 1024x640 laptop needed it, and five classes alone was already cut off top and bottom there. Screens it fits are untouched.
- **The mascot is the splash screen's chalkboard, cheering**: wood frame, green board, a chalk smile with its eyes shut, both arms thrown up for the "Yes!" and a few stars. The artwork is the teacher's own, made in Gemini and traced to SVG in Canva. The whole character is the browser-tab icon (`public/favicon.svg`); the close-up tile (one arm, the frame's corner) is the home-screen icon (`apple-touch-icon.png`), on its own green because iOS fills see-through corners with black. A simpler redraw was made for tab size first and replaced by his: at 32px his reads fine, and at 16px any tab icon is a coloured square with a face. The speech bubbles stay as the name on the splash; the mascot is the face of the app where there is no room for words.
- Because it's designed to share the screen rather than own it, **coexisting with the lesson app is the core premise**, not a nice-to-have.

## Deliberately not building

- **Reasons / labels on points** (ClassDojo-style). Teachers and admins report the taxonomy is the cumbersome part — it inserts a decision between the moment and the reward, which is fatal for something whose value is immediacy.
- **Further timer investment.** The smartboard has a timer, so do Gynzy and Classroom Screen. Ours is nice and costs nothing to keep, but nobody switches apps for a timer. **One exception, made on purpose: the dial** (Settled mechanics, "Timer"). It isn't a nicer clock: a child who can't yet read 4:53 can read a red that shrinks, and the teacher asked for it after using Gynzy's every day.
- **Whiteboard, lesson building, noise meter, AI images.** Gynzy's territory; we'd always be behind.
- **Student self-check-in for attendance.** Thirty kids tapping in creates a queue at the board; one teacher scanning the room takes ten seconds.
- **Fly-from-desks transition** for the flip deck. The slide is enough.

## Open questions

- **Are per-student point tallies actually wanted?** Derek's own practice is whole-class — marbles all land in one jar, no individual scorekeeping. The app is individual-first with the class meter riding along. That may be right for other teachers, or it may be the ClassDojo instinct sneaking in.
- Which existing features need tweaking. Derek reports several do; specifics need real classroom use to pin down.

## Settled mechanics

- **The app never scrolls.** Not the board, not a screen that slides over it, not the side panel. Everything is laid out to fit the viewport, and when there is more than fits, the layout adapts (fewer rows, smaller chips) rather than the page growing. A modal may scroll its *body*, but the modal itself always fits — it is capped at 90% of the viewport with a scrolling body, so its title and edges never leave the screen. This is a rule, not a preference: a teacher at a board never scrolls to find a control.

- **Screen sizes and devices: it fits any screen from 1024x500 up, with no setting changed** (2026-10-02, the teacher's rule: "it will be used on various smartboards and screens, and must need no tweaking of Windows or browser settings"). The teacher's own board is a 4K panel with Windows at 300%, which, after Chrome's tabs, bookmarks bar and the taskbar, gives the page **1280x559** - shorter than anything it had been checked at, and the side panel ran off the bottom: by 106px with the timer controls open, 72px with the class list open, 8px on the flip cards. Turning Chrome's zoom or Windows' scaling down would have fixed his board and no one else's, so the app fits itself instead. **Nothing opens by pushing the side panel down**: the class list opens over the panel like a menu (a tap anywhere else closes it), and the timer controls are a drawer that **slides out from under the clock** and over Pickers & Points, in the timer's colour, while the time is being set; a tap on the timer slides it back, as before. The drawer's window starts tucked under the clock's rounded bottom and clips what is in it, so the controls come out from beneath the clock; it is one CSS slide the graphics chip runs. The first version faded in over the clock for a moment and then popped down, and on the board it looked broken (2026-10-03). **The side panel tightens itself to its height** (`hooks/useFitToHeight.ts`): drawn, measured, and if it overflows, one step tighter, before anything is painted - the gaps first, then the buttons' padding, then the dial, then the Pick All row. Text never shrinks for it, and a screen it fitted is drawn exactly as before. It measures again when something adds a line (a selection, the flip cards, the Saved mark), when the window is resized and when the fonts arrive. **Checked** at 1024x640, 1280x800 and 1920x1080 as before, and at the room real screens leave a page: 1024x500 (the floor), 1280x559 (his board), 1366x620, 1440x780, 1536x700 (1080p at 125%, common on laptops), 1920x940 and 2560x1300 - in every theme, both timer faces, with the timer controls and the class list open, a student selected and the flip cards up. The walkthrough's "nothing scrolls" checks the same list. The two old exceptions at 1024x640 (the timer controls with a student selected) are gone. **It is not designed for phones or tablets**: they can technically open it in any browser (iOS Safari, Android Chrome, etc.), but the UI breaks—text is tiny, buttons are too small, and layouts overflow. A phone-first responsive redesign would be a major rebuild. A **phone UI is planned separately** (see "Phone as a remote control" in Wish list) for admin tasks and in-class shortcuts, not the full board. The app's core product is the smartboard experience; the phone extension is convenience and control from elsewhere in the room.

- **Smooth on the board: what moves, and how.** The teacher found the whole app laggy and choppy, at school and at home on any connection. It was never the internet: once the app is open nothing waits on it (each picture and sound is fetched the first time it's used). It was how hard the app made the device work, found by driving the production build at a board's speed - processor slowed 4x, 4K-like pixel density - and counting frames. Two causes. **Frosted glass**: the side panel, the goal bar, the flip-card and group toolbars, the dialog dim and the status picker blurred what was behind them, and a browser redoes every blur on every frame anything on screen moves. On a 4K board that halved the frame rate, and the dial timer, moving every frame inside the blurred panel, ran at 35. They are the same see-through colours without the blur; the dialog dim went from 40% to 50% and the status picker's to 60% so they still stand apart from the board. The only blur left is Delete Class's small safety cover, whose job is to hide the button under it. **Animations run by the main processor**: framer-motion moved things frame by frame in JavaScript - nine sparkles per point, stars swirling round a picked desk, thirty cards dealt, thirty name chips dealt, a spring on every button press, the flip clock's leaves every second - and made the page be measured on every render for a side-panel glide used once a term. **What went**, with the teacher asking for anything not really needed to go: the stars round a picked student or row (the picked desk now has a still amber outline, the pickers' colour everywhere else, and an inked black one in Comic Book, where amber disappears into the yellow ground), the +1 sparkles on the goal bar and, once it was live, the "+1" that rose off the coin on every point, and the one that rose off the floating window's +1 button (the count popping and the coin moving already say it), the brightness filter on a flickering desk or group card, and the side panel's glide when it changes sides. **What stayed, moved to CSS or `element.animate`**, which the graphics chip runs by itself, and looks the same: the goal bar's coin, fill, count pop and chest rattle; every button's press; the flip clock's leaves; the flip-card deal, turn, lift and fade; and the group deal, where each chip remembers where it was drawn and glides home from there - one small glide for gathering, dealing, moving a name and the chips closing up behind it. The dial redraws only as often as its edge moves, every half degree (about 12 times a second for a 1-minute timer, once a second for 20 minutes), not 60 times a second. And the windows no longer build their contents while closed: Class Settings built the roster and a month of attendance on every desk tap. Measured at 4K-like density with the processor slowed 4x, frames per second before → after: dial timer 35 → 57, flipping cards 30 → 52, +1 39 → 56, picking a student 46 → 58, dealing groups 33 → 50, opening flip cards 38 → 50, tapping desks 50 → 56. What is left is a one-off pause as the flip-card and group screens are first built. The rules this leaves are in CLAUDE.md; `scripts/frame-check.mjs` repeats the measurement.

- **The side panel grows with a tall screen.** Its text is sized in vmin, so on a 1080-tall board the text grew while the panel stayed 16rem: the class name was cut to "4B Eng…" and Swap Seats ran out past the panel's edge. The panel is now 30vmin, held between 16rem and 21rem. That is unchanged up to about 850px tall, which is where the text starts growing, and in step with it from there to where the text stops, so what fits at 1280x800 fits on a bigger board. The desks at 1920x1080 are a little narrower for it.
- **Elementary's everyday buttons are pale, with purple words; yellow means a point.** From the zoom-out review (2026-09-30): every button in the default theme was the same yellow - nine on the side panel alone, and every one in every window - so nothing stood out and the eye had nowhere to land. Now they are a pale lilac, purple still means on, and the one yellow button left is **+**, on the side panel and on a group card, so a hand finds it without looking. The other themes already had quiet buttons and keep them; Comic Book stays loud on purpose. **Pick All, − and + grow with the screen**: they were the smallest buttons on the panel, 38px, while a third of it sat empty on a big board. They are 38px at 640 tall, as before (the panel is tightest there), about 50px at 800 and about 70px at 1080, with the words and signs growing too. Chosen from a before-and-after mock-up of the real app.
- **The class name has the top row to itself**, with only the class switcher's arrow and a **gear for Class Settings** beside it. Attendance used to share that row and left the name about 100px, or 67px with the arrow, so "Grade 4 English" read "Grade …". Every idea that gave the name its own row pushed the whole panel down, so the top two rows swapped contents instead and nothing moved: Class Settings became a gear beside the class name, the way the pickers' settings are a gear beside theirs, and **Attendance went beside Swap Seats**, where the word Settings was. Those two are the desk modes - each turns a desk tap into something else and only one can be on - so the one that's on lights up next to the one greyed out. The name now gets about 160px at 1280x800 with two classes. Checked in the real faces (Andika, Cabin Sketch, Bangers): "Grade 4 English" is full size in every theme at 1024x640, 1280x800 and 1920x1080, where before it was cut with two classes in all of them, and the Pickers & Points box sits 1-5px higher (2px lower in Comic Book at 1920, whose name is now full size). Settings lost its word; the gear says Class Settings on hover, and only the teacher uses it. **A name that still doesn't fit shrinks, by up to a quarter, before it truncates**: "Kindergarten Phonics" is at 83-98% depending on the theme, and uncut. It is measured in the page rather than with `fitText`, because Chalkboard and Comic Book letter the class name in faces wider than Andika.

- **Desks: thirty, and a seventh column for a class of more than thirty (35 at most).** Six across and five deep fits most classes and looks right, so it stays exactly as it is; the seventh column appears on the right only when the roster passes thirty (or someone is still sitting in it), with no setting to find. The new desks are numbered 30-34 on the end of the seating list rather than re-numbering the grid seven wide, because re-numbering would have scrambled every saved seating chart; saves from before are padded with five empty desks on load. A 30-desk board was checked pixel-for-pixel against the old one. In a big class every desk is a seventh narrower and the names shrink with it (they are sized to the desk). Seat Class fills the seven columns bottom row first like the six; Pick Row picks from seven rows (only ever one with somebody in it today - a class that doesn't fill the grid, or a row whose students are all away, could land on a row of nobody); Split by Rows can make seven groups; flip cards lay out seven wide past thirty (35 is exactly five full rows). **Known tight spot:** Pairs with 35 students makes 17 groups, which at 1024x640 squeezes the cards to six narrow columns, small names and a cut-off "Group…" title. Fine at 1280x800 and up. (A second tight spot, the timer's controls with a student selected at 1024x640, went when the controls began opening over the panel - see Screen sizes.)
- **Room layouts: Rows, Pairs, Rows of 3, Tables of 4, and Tables of 5 two ways** (the fifth desk above the four, or below them). Picked per class from little drawings in Class Settings → Class, like the themes, since classes can meet in different rooms. The teacher's school sets its rooms out in rows, but not every school does. They were chosen from a mock-up that drew each layout with the app's own desks at board size: **every layout keeps six desks across**, because a name is sized by its desk's width, so names stay today's size everywhere; the tables pay in height instead (a sixth row, pictures about 75-80% of today's). Tables of 6 and L-shaped groups of 3 were drawn and left out; the L needed eight desks across and cost the names a quarter. Pairs and Rows of 3 push desks together (3px) with aisles between; tables sit in three columns of tables with aisles all round, and a table of five's end desk is centred on the table. - **Picker sounds: a soft pop on every other flash, then a gentle chime** - for every picker (Pick Student, Pick Row, the group pickers and the floating class goal). The teacher chose the shape by ear on a listening page ("Floating Pick Sounds", a Claude artifact) that played a whole pick three ways: the old sound, a dry 3.5kHz tick on every flash at full volume with a pop-and-sparkle landing, was "too jarring" and he wanted it out of the app altogether; the soft pop at half volume on every flash was good but "still a lot of pops"; the same pop (pop.mp3) on every other flash, with a soft ding to land, was the one he liked. The desk or name still changes every 90ms; only the sound is halved. **Then it was tuned on the board**, in a standalone preview of the app with an example class (a Claude artifact, "Softer Picker Preview"): at a third of full volume the pops needed the speakers all the way up, and the single 1.3kHz ding, which started at full strength, came out "loud and abrupt" after them. Measured offline, the quiet pops and the ding were about equally loud, and 1.3kHz is where classroom speakers and ears are strongest while 500Hz is near the bottom of what the speakers do. So the pops went up to 80% (8 dB louder) and the ding became a chime: G, C and E rolled upward 70ms apart, each fading in over 40ms and ringing about a second, with a faint octave for shimmer - its loudest 50ms about 2 dB under the ding's. Heard again in the preview on the board: "MUCH better", and it went live. tick.mp3 was deleted.
- **Point sound: an arcade coin, one sound for every point** - the class goal meter moving up (points from the desks, +1 in the floating window, Everyone +1) and +1 on a group card, which used to have a ping of its own. The old pings (two quick sine tones, the meter's topping out near 2kHz, the sharpest spot on a board speaker) were jarring, and the teacher asked for a coin "like in Mario, or some sort of chime". A listening page ("Point Award Sounds", a Claude artifact) played four at matched loudness (within 0.3 dB of the old ping), one point or a quick run of three: the old ping, a coin, a small bell, and the single marble from the Marble Sound Lab tuned lower. He chose the coin: "great". It is a short G then a C that rings about half a second, in a triangle wave with a faint octave on top - a coin in spirit, not Nintendo's own sound, and rounder and lower than a console's buzzy square wave. The marble lost to it for points; the Lab's other marble ideas (the pour, the swirl) stay parked.
- **Minus sound: "Negative reverberate", one low note that drops and fades, at 43% volume.** The recording before it was the teacher's own earlier pick, but it played at full volume, about 14 dB louder than the coin (measured offline, loudest 50ms: -8.9 dB against -22.7), and the room heard it as a blast. A listening page ("Minus Point Sounds", a Claude artifact) offered it turned down, the coin played backwards, a soft buzzer and a wooden plonk, and he liked none of them. He then sent nine recordings of his own. They were measured rather than heard: a low beep (99% under 250Hz) and a bongo were ruled out as too low for a board speaker, a punch because a hitting sound for a child losing a point is the wrong picture, and two pops because the picker already pops. Of three trimmed and levelled on a second page ("Your Minus Sounds"), he chose Negative reverberate. **It breaks the keep-above-400Hz rule on purpose**: nearly all of it is 280-400Hz, and it was picked by ear anyway. If it goes quiet on the board, its volume is the first knob. It is levelled by A-weighting rather than plain loudness, because the ear hears a low note as quieter: about 1.3 dB under the coin to the ear. The file's 71ms of silence at the front is skipped so it lands with the tap, and the synthesised fallback (for when the recording won't play) was turned down to match; at full it was the old blast again. **Listening-page trap**: both pages first played none of the recordings, because the artifact viewer blocks `fetch()`, even of a `data:` address. - **Flip Cards: one tap, one meaning, nothing timed.** A student comes up and taps a card; whoever it reveals has the turn, and the side panel's +/- go to them - no selecting a card first, because a tap on a face-up card is how it gets put away. The newest card glows amber; earlier face-up cards dim but stay readable, and stay up. Tapping a faded card gives it the turn back (a spelling bee's "try again") and fades whoever had it. **Only the glowing card can be put away**, and what that tap does is the one mode, **Flip Back** or **Discard**, and it sits on the flip toolbar rather than in settings because it changes what the tap means. Timed flip-back and set-aside were built and taken out: the teacher, not a clock, decides when a turn ends. So discarding a faded card takes two deliberate taps - wake it, then put it away - which is the protection a double-tap-to-discard would have given, without asking for a gesture the smart board fakes on its own. Putting away the active card leaves nobody active and +/- greyed rather than quietly handing the points back to someone who already had their turn. The window is out of the children's reach, so the guard is only there for the board's ghost touch, which comes within a split second: +1 straight after +1 waits only 0.2 s, so four or five deliberate taps a second all count. The long guard stays wherever a tap changes what's under the finger: Pick, Celebrate, a picked name, the size button, and a +1 that follows any of them. Separate taps, not press-and-hold: a finger held down on a Windows touch board is a right-click. On the board: tap slowly a few times, and if one tap ever gives two points, raise it to 0.3 s. The side panel's + and a group card's + have no guard at all, so they already answered every tap. **The flicker runs on the floating window's clock**: the app's page is behind the lesson, and Chrome slows a hidden page's timers to about one a second, so the pick is paced by the float's own timer (`pickStudent(clock)`). If the float closes mid-flash that clock stops with it, so the pick is cancelled rather than left flashing forever. **It shrinks to a strip in one tap**, because Derek wanted it see-through so it wouldn't block the lesson, and a web page can't make its window see-through. A shrink button at the end of the meter row turns the window into one row, 240x62 (the smallest Chrome will resize it to; 240x52 only when opening): the count, +1 and Pick, and a grow button that puts it back at whatever size it was before. In the strip the class name, fill and chest give way, Pick loses its icon, a pick is a name the height of the strip, and Celebrate reads "Open the app" once tapped. The layout follows the window's own height (under 120px is the strip), so dragging an edge small gives the strip too. The resize is `resizeTo` from the tap, measured against the window's title bar and edges, which differ between computers; the window keeps its top-left corner where it was. No button back to the full app yet: the window's title bar has the browser's own. Flip Cards, Group Activity and Settings stay in the app: they need the whole board. The clock stays out: the board's own toolbar has one.
- **Two knobs on the goal, not one.** Stars convert to class points at a teacher-set rate, and the goal is counted in class points. A divisor doesn't change *pacing* — the goal alone already does, since "10 stars each, goal 8" is arithmetically "goal 80" — it changes the number the class looks at. 5/8 is a jar filling; 48/80 is a score. Leftover stars are banked, so awarding one at a time eventually counts for as much as awarding them all at once. Default is 1:1, so classes saved before this behave exactly as they did.
- **Resetting stars and resetting the meter are separate actions.** They were briefly one, on the grounds that both describe the same term — but that argues for making both easy, not for forcing a teacher who wants one to destroy the other.
- **Class Settings is three tabs: Students, Class and Google.** It was one page grouped by row rather than meaning, and the roster - the part used most - got a row and a half of it on a big class. The Google tab came with Drive ("Google Drive and Sheets"); the two below are as they were. **Students** (opens first from the side panel): Add a Student, then the roster, which takes all the height left, with Import CSV then **View / Edit Attendance** on its header (a CSV can still be dropped on the roster; the big drop box went, since on a board it was tapped anyway). **Importing skips anyone already on the roster** (same name and homeroom), so bringing in a file a second time, after fixing a line in it, adds only who is new rather than doubling the class; the same file can be chosen twice in a row, which used to do nothing at all. A roster edit that wipes out a name keeps the old one, and deleting a student takes them out of the last groups and the attendance record too. **There is no roster export**: the teacher saw no need for it, so the button and its code went. The foot runs in the order a class is set up: **Student Avatars** with a one-line note of what it does, then **Mix Up Seats**, Unseat All and Seat Students together. Mix Up Seats (from the zoom-out review) is a new seating plan in one tap, behind a confirm because the old plan can't be brought back: everyone on the roster is dealt into random seats, back rows first like Seat Students, the window closes on the new plan and a riffle plays. Before it, a fresh plan meant Unseat All and then Swap Seats two at a time. Unseat All sits beside Seat Students because they are opposites. Sharing the foot row costs the roster no height, which a row of its own would have (about one student fewer at 1024x640). The attendance button is named for what it opens - the record - because attendance is *taken* on the seating chart with the side panel's button, not here. **The gender box reads "Boy or Girl" until one is picked**, in the placeholder's grey: it has no label of its own and "Unspecified" didn't say what it was for. Boy and Girl are the only choices; a student can't be set back to not chosen, though a CSV without a gender column still brings them in that way. **Class**: New Class and Delete Class together at the top (the delete cover's note has room there; it used to run into New Class at 1920 wide), then the class name and the theme (marked as changing every class, since it does). **A class made from the splash opens on this tab** (Create My First Class and New Class alike), because the first thing a new class needs is its name. The class name belongs to the class, not the students, which is why it moved here from beside Attendance. **Themes are picked from small drawings** - a strip of side panel and six desks - in each theme's real colours: the theme tokens also apply under `[data-theme-preview]`, so a change to a theme shows in its thumbnail by itself, plus a mark or two per theme (Comic Book's ink and dots, Chalkboard's wooden frame). **Student Avatars moved to the Students tab**, because it acts on every student, and it is named that rather than Class Avatars for the same reason. A strip of the class's first ten avatars beside it was taken out: a short note saying what the button does is enough. What space is left on the Class tab stays empty rather than being filled for the sake of it. The window holds one height so switching tabs doesn't resize it.
- **Points settings live in Pickers & Points**, not Class Settings, with per-student editing in the roster (the only per-student surface). Class Settings is for the class's composition and appearance.
- **Flip-card picker**: slides over the desk grid rather than taking the full screen, so the side panel's point buttons and the goal meter stay live. Seated students only. Its own round, independent of Pick Student's history — setting a card aside *is* the no-repeat mechanism. Gender colouring on card backs is cosmetic (all students dealt), and flipping awards no points automatically.
- **Avatars**: Flaticon sticker packs (paid subscription), imported by `import-stickers.py` into `public/avatars/stickers/<theme>/<pose>.svg`. This replaced the Google Flow set and fixed the proportion drift outright — every sticker is drawn on the same 512×512 canvas, so nothing needs per-image CSS. A student with no avatar gets a character derived from their id, so a freshly imported class already looks varied. **Every pose on offer is one a teacher is happy to see on a desk.** The teacher went through all 468 stickers on a review page and took out 138: crying, sad, angry, sick, hurt and scary ones, plus poses that read as grumpy, shocked, or odd for a classroom (skulls, swords, a beer, piles of money). They are listed in `stickers/removed.txt` rather than deleted, so the paid packs stay whole and re-running the import never brings them back. Lion, Ghost and Wolf lost their automatic resting face (the angry wolf was every unpicked wolf's face), so the import names a smiling one for each. A student who was wearing a removed pose keeps their character on its resting face rather than turning into another animal.
- **Bulk avatar assignment**: Class Settings → Class → Student Avatars. Scope (everyone / boys / girls) × poses (mixed / same for all), then one tap applies; tapping the same character again re-rolls. Immediate rather than a confirm step — the undo is tapping something else.
- **Typeface**: Andika, app-wide. Chosen for its single-storey lowercase `a`, which is how the students are taught to write it; Comic Sans was rejected. Lexend was a close second. `lib/fitText.ts` measures in Andika because it picks one name size for a whole class — measuring in the wrong face would size every desk wrong.
- **Desk layout**: rounded top, square bottom. **An empty desk is just the faded tile**: the word "Empty" on every one was one more word on a board already full of names. The points badge sits top-right over the avatar's empty corner (only with Show Stars on Desks on; see Points), leaving the whole bottom row to the name. **The homeroom number shows after the name, small and quiet, and only where two students share a name** (below). One name size per class, computed from the longest name, so no student's desk reads smaller than the rest. **Names use their own colour, `--desk-name`, softer than the theme's text colour.** On a smartboard the names were the loudest thing on screen — big, bold and near-black at about 14:1 contrast, against 6:1 for the app's own button text — and read as glaring. Size was not the problem: a smaller name was still harsh, and a lighter weight is a big jump because Andika has only regular and bold, and thin strokes are the first thing a projector washes out. So the names stay bold and full size, and Light and Elementary each use a dark tinted from their own palette at about 9:1, well clear of the 7:1 readability bar. Dark (white on dark) and Comic Book (ink on purpose) keep their card text colour, and Chalkboard writes them in chalk white on a desk a shade deeper than the board, also about 9:1.
- **Themes**: five, chosen in Class Settings → Class — Light, Dark, Chalkboard, Elementary (the default until a teacher picks otherwise; only a pick in the picker is remembered, never the OS preference) and Comic Book. School Colors, built from the Yuteh crest, was removed at the teacher's request. Sky Blue made way for Chalkboard, in its slot in the picker: Light, Sky Blue and Elementary were all light and blue, so it was the one most like the others. A teacher who had a removed theme picked lands on Elementary. Three of the five are pure CSS-variable swaps and can stay that way. **Comic Book is the exception and is allowed to be**: what makes a page look printed is ink weight, a halftone lattice and hard shadows, none of which is a colour. So it carries a structural block in `index.css` alongside its tokens, under two rules that keep that from spreading. It only reaches for hooks that already exist — `data-slot` on the shadcn primitives, plus `data-ink` on the three app containers that had none (canvas, panel, desk) — and it never touches anything that affects layout: every outline is a `box-shadow` ring rather than a border, so switching themes cannot reflow the board. Bangers letters the chrome; student names stay in Andika, because an all-caps display face is the wrong thing for a roster of real names read across a room. Colour varies per desk through a rotating five, carried in a caption bar across the panel's top rather than a wash or a centred glow — both of those put saturated colour behind the student's name. Texture alternates between a halftone lattice and diagonal speed ruling, so neighbouring panels aren't printed the same way. **Chalkboard is the second exception, under the same two rules.** It came from the teacher's picture of a green classroom board with coloured chalk doodles and a wooden ledge. The board is chalkboard green with chalk dust (the splash screen's grain) and a wooden frame, drawn as an inset shadow inside the canvas's own padding so nothing moves. Desks are slate tiles a shade deeper than the board, outlined in soft white chalk: the side panel buttons' chalk, drawn a little heavier, with the homeroom number in the same chalk a step quieter than the name. The first version gave each desk one of five chalk colours on a diagonal, like Comic Book's caption bars, and on a real board thirty coloured outlines were a distraction from the names. It is a dark theme underneath, so the `dark:` variant covers it as well as Dark. The splash screen's chalk face (Cabin Sketch) letters the class name, panel headings and dialog titles; names stay in Andika for the same reason as Comic Book. Switched-on buttons are yellow chalk, dialogs get the wooden frame, and the flip deck and group cards get the dust through `data-ink="board"`. Flip card backs and group colours stay as they are in every theme.

- **Group Activity**: one button on the side panel, one modal, one screen. The modal is all buttons, no typing: how many groups (2–6), groups of (pairs, 3s, 4s, 5s), or split by boys/girls or rows, each captioned with what it makes of *this* class today ("7–8 each", "15 groups") and greyed out with the reason when it can't. Tapping one deals: the chips gather into a stack in the middle of the board and are dealt out one at a time into colour-banded cards, with the side panel's pickers and +/- standing down the way they do for Swap Seats — the timer stays live. Building groups by hand is deliberately not a mode (thirty students is sixty taps); fix-ups are tap a chip, then tap the card it belongs in — anywhere on the card, chips included. Dragging was built and taken out: on a touch board it fights with scrolling, and one way to do a thing is easier to teach than two. Chips are name and homeroom number only, no avatars, **one size for the whole class regardless of how many groups there are**, all the same width (set by the longest name, like the desks' one name size), so they line up in rows; a name past the cap shrinks inside its chip. Cards fit their contents and every card in a deal shares the tallest one's height, centred on the board, so empty space is around the cards rather than inside them. **The board never scrolls**: a planner knows every card's dimensions, so it picks the column count that fits at full-size chips, then tightens the card's chrome (three densities: padding, light size and button size give way in that order), and only when nothing fits does it shrink the chips a step (all of them, so one size for the class holds) and try again — names are what has to be read from the back of the room, so they are the last thing to give. The chrome is computed from the classes, not measured, since the plan decides which density renders; the frame is a real border, since a ring outside the card was clipped wherever it met the board's edge. Leftovers spread into existing groups rather than forming a group of one. Each card carries its own score, with the same +/- as the side panel. **Exit Group Activity** is the one way out: with nothing scored it just closes; with points on the board it asks whether they go out now or wait with the groups — handing thirty students their stars is not a side effect of leaving a screen. Points go where the toggle at the bottom of the modal says, remembered per class: a star per point to every member, or one class point per group point onto the goal meter. Choosing Class Goal with no goal switches the class goal on right there (greying the button out sent the teacher looking for a bug). Groups survive exit, so "Continue with Last Groups" picks the same teams up tomorrow, scores included if they were kept; anyone no longer seated drops out on the way; a shuffle keeps each card's identity by position so only the chips move. Colours are fixed across themes (they *are* the group's identity); everything else follows the theme, like Flip Cards. **No group is red, amber, orange, green or grey**, because those are the statuses and the status is what the room scans for: the set used to include the exact red of Help and green of Done, so Group 1 always wore a red band while working happily, and "look for the red card" stopped working. Twelve colours now (blue, pink, teal, violet, brown and charcoal first, so the usual two to six groups are six different families); pairs of a full 35 make 17 groups, so the last few repeat. The Girls group went from rose, which read as Help, to pink. Groups saved in an old colour are recoloured by position when they come back. Names are Group 1, 2, 3 (or Boys and Girls) and can't be changed, so nothing in Group Activity asks anyone to type.
  - **Status.** Groups are for projects as much as games, so every card carries one of four states: Working, Help, Ready, Done. Ready and Done are deliberately different — Ready means a group has stopped and is waiting for the teacher, Done means there is nothing left to do. **The status colours the card, it is not a widget on the card.** The body and the points row both take a **strong** wash in the status colour and the band and frame keep the group's own colour, so scanning the room is "look for the red card", which is the red, amber and green cups on a group's table. The wash is deliberately heavy: the first attempt used a 16% tint and from the back of a room it was indistinguishable from the next card. Name chips sit on the plain card colour so they stay crisp on top of it. The small status chip in the band wears the status colour too, ringed in the band's own text colour so it still separates when a red group is the one asking for help — a faded chip said nothing at a distance and the chip and the body should say the same thing. Working has no wash at all: if every card were tinted the board would go one flat colour and a single red card would stop standing out. Help pulses a soft ring, and it is the only status that moves, because it is the only one asking for you now. Icons are Pencil, question mark, thumbs up and tick, chosen to be read without English — a raised hand turns to mush at small sizes and a flag has to be taught. Help, Ready and Done chime, switchable in the Group Activity modal. Statuses survive Exit and Continue and reset with a new deal.
  - **Group names cannot be edited.** They are Group 1, 2, 3 or Boys and Girls, and that is enough — a teacher who wants "Red Team" can say it. Renaming existed briefly and its field stretched from the name to the status chip, which mostly served as a way for a student to open a dialog by accident. Removing it removed the accident and a whole modal.
    - **The first version was a strip of four lights on the card and it was wrong twice over.** The lit light expanded in place, so its position moved from card to card and a board of fifteen never lined up; and the strip had a fixed 200px minimum the layout planner never reserved, so at fifteen groups it was clipped at both ends. Cards were optimised one at a time and never looked at fifteen side by side.
  - **Choosing a status is a panel low on the screen, not buttons on the card.** A card in the top row of a smartboard is two metres up and a small card would mean small buttons, which is worst for the shortest student. So the board dims, the card flies down to a fixed spot and four large buttons sit beneath it in the bottom third. One row, left to right, in the order work goes: a two by two grid leaves a child guessing whether to read across or down. Every button wears its status colour, so the code is taught each time. **No close button and no instructions**: the group's current status is one of the four and is shown as already chosen, so a child who opened it by accident taps the lit one and nothing happens. Tapping outside closes it too. The panel is solid over a board dimmed by 60% rather than frosted — the deep dim is what makes it feel lifted, and two translucent layers read as muddy on a projector that has already eaten the contrast. It was a blur, which a 4K board redid on every frame anything moved behind it (see Smooth on the board).
  - **The points row is sized by the card's width, not by the density.** Density is chosen to make cards fit by *height*, so a board that went to eight columns could leave a card half as wide carrying a row built for a wide one — which is how the plus and minus ended up clipped at the card's edges. Widths and icon sizes now come from the card's width while heights still come from the density, so the planner's arithmetic stays true.
  - **Picking, during a group activity, reuses the side panel's two picker buttons.** Pick Row becomes **Pick Group** while the cards are up — a row and a group are both "a set of students", so the button keeps its meaning and only what counts as a set changes — and Pick Student flashes across the name chips instead of the desks. The pickers are a family and a teacher already looks in that box for them; putting one in the group toolbar would split them across two places and add a sixth control to a row that wraps. They were greyed out before because the desk picker lights up desks, which are behind the cards and so invisible here, so `useGroupPicker` is a separate hook at the same pace and with the same sounds, pointed at the cards. The winner is ringed in amber, the one colour that is never a group's and never a status. It honours the same Allow Repeats setting, tracked per activity and reset on a new deal, and a landed pick clears on a tap anywhere. **A picked group keeps Pick Student inside it**, the way a picked row does on the desks (2026-10-03, the teacher's call): once Pick Group lands, Pick Student reads **Pick from This Group** and draws only from that group, whose card keeps its amber ring while the others stay faded. A tap on the board lets the group go and the button reads Pick Student again; so does a new deal, and so does the group running out of students who haven't had a turn (with Allow Repeats off), one pick before it would stop meaning it, as Pick from This Row does.
    - Two framer-motion traps here, both worth remembering: it will not clear an inline `box-shadow` it has set, so the winner's ring had to be a class or every past winner stayed ringed; and on an element with a `layoutId` the shared-layout pass writes an inline `opacity` that no utility class can beat, so a dimmed chip's opacity has to go through `animate`. (The chips no longer use framer at all - see Smooth on the board - but the traps stand for anything that still does.)
  - **The riffle belongs to the Shuffle button.** A first deal played it too, so making groups sounded like two sounds landing on top of each other; there is nothing to shuffle when the cards are being made for the first time.
  - **Reset Points** sits in the group toolbar, greyed until something has been scored, behind a confirm. It zeroes the scores and leaves the groups alone, for running the same teams through a second round.
  - **Settings and Swap Seats stand down during a group activity.** Both rearrange the class underneath a running activity, and Swap Seats acts on desks that are behind the cards. Swap Seats is out whenever the cards are up, flip cards included (2026-10-03: it acts on the desks, so it is only for the seating chart); Settings goes out when the board is locked, which is when students are the ones standing at it.
  - **Lock** is for students coming up to the board. It freezes what would destroy work: moving a name, New Groups, Shuffle, Exit and the side panel's button. **Points and status stay live**, because awarding a point for being on task mid activity is normal and unlocking to do it would be backwards. It is a courtesy lock, not a PIN.

- **Deployment**: pushes to `main` auto-deploy to https://a102032.github.io/seating/. The workflow curls the published URL afterwards and fails if it isn't serving the built bundle — Pages served the repo's raw `index.html` for several runs while every step reported green. `build-artifact.py` bundles a standalone copy for the Claude artifact preview.

## Known loose ends

- **Numbered sticker packs have no pose names.** 13 of the 20 packs are numbered (`owl-002`), so `absent` can't be detected for them. Their glum poses were taken out by eye (see Avatars), so bulk assignment no longer needs names to avoid them.
- **Andika ships only weights 400 and 700.** `font-semibold` renders identically to `font-bold` throughout the app. Nothing looks broken, but the type has one less step than the design assumed.
