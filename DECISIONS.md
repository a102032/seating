# Decisions

Running record of what we've settled and why, so the reasoning survives between sessions.
Newest thinking at the top of each section. Last updated 2026-09-28. Loaded into every new Claude Code session through CLAUDE.md.

## What this app is

- **A seating chart, attendance and points tool that runs *alongside* Gynzy / NaniBox / Classroom Screen** — not a replacement for them. Gynzy already does whiteboard, lessons, timers and noise meters well; its seating chart and attendance are weak. That gap is the product.
- **Target user: teachers who want marble-jar dynamics but can't have the jar.** Derek's room runs on a physical marble jar, a per-class jar, and a battery wall that fills toward a treasure chest. Colleagues like watching it work and default to ClassDojo instead because they can't replicate it.
- **Deliberately fewer features than ClassDojo**: no parent accounts, no behaviour taxonomy, no setup. The things people complain about are the things we leave out. That's a position, not a limitation.
- Because it's designed to share the screen rather than own it, **coexisting with the lesson app is the core premise**, not a nice-to-have.

## Next up

- **Use it in a real class before building anything else.** It's live and working. Three lessons will replace a speculative bug list with a real one, and will answer whether the floating-window idea is genuinely useful or just looked good in someone else's app.
## Parked — revisit, don't rebuild

- **Marble sounds** (`design/marble-sounds.html`, published as its own artifact). Four candidates mapped to classroom moments. Derek: "gets so close to the actual sound." Undecided whether they go in the app; the synthesis ports straight into `lib/sound.ts` if so.
  - The insight behind it: his marble swirl is a *command* (it changes the room's behaviour), where all our current sounds are *feedback* (they confirm something happened). The award sound should carry across a room, not confirm a click.
  - Attention-getter sound boards already exist (Classroom Screen, Class123), so a sound *library* is commodity. What isn't commodity is the reward system's own sound being the cue.
- **Picture-in-Picture floating window** — a small always-on-top window (Chrome/Edge) holding the timer and a compact scrollable name list with inline +/−. Roughly a day's work vs. weeks for Electron, and it tests the actual hypothesis. `useCountdown` already computes from a stored end-timestamp, so background throttling won't corrupt the clock.
- **Electron port** — deferred. Only it can give a true borderless floating icon pinned to the corner. If PiP proves the idea useful, this becomes a polish exercise worth doing; if not, it's saved entirely. Electron over Tauri when we do: predictability and prior art matter more than footprint, since none of it can be tested from here.

## Deliberately not building

- **Reasons / labels on points** (ClassDojo-style). Teachers and admins report the taxonomy is the cumbersome part — it inserts a decision between the moment and the reward, which is fatal for something whose value is immediacy.
- **Further timer investment.** The smartboard has a timer, so do Gynzy and Classroom Screen. Ours is nice and costs nothing to keep, but nobody switches apps for a timer.
- **Whiteboard, lesson building, noise meter, AI images.** Gynzy's territory; we'd always be behind.
- **Student self-check-in for attendance.** Thirty kids tapping in creates a queue at the board; one teacher scanning the room takes ten seconds.
- **Fly-from-desks transition** for the flip deck. The slide is enough.

## Open questions

- **Are per-student point tallies actually wanted?** Derek's own practice is whole-class — marbles all land in one jar, no individual scorekeeping. The app is individual-first with the class meter riding along. That may be right for other teachers, or it may be the ClassDojo instinct sneaking in.
- Which existing features need tweaking. Derek reports several do; specifics need real classroom use to pin down.

## Settled mechanics

- **The app never scrolls.** Not the board, not a screen that slides over it, not the side panel. Everything is laid out to fit the viewport, and when there is more than fits, the layout adapts (fewer rows, smaller chips) rather than the page growing. A modal may scroll its *body*, but the modal itself always fits — it is capped at 90% of the viewport with a scrolling body, so its title and edges never leave the screen. This is a rule, not a preference: a teacher at a board never scrolls to find a control.

- **The side panel grows with a tall screen.** Its text is sized in vmin, so on a 1080-tall board the text grew while the panel stayed 16rem: the class name was cut to "4B Eng…" and Swap Seats ran out past the panel's edge. The panel is now 30vmin, held between 16rem and 21rem. That is unchanged up to about 850px tall, which is where the text starts growing, and in step with it from there to where the text stops, so what fits at 1280x800 fits on a bigger board. The desks at 1920x1080 are a little narrower for it. **A class name that still doesn't fit shrinks, by up to a quarter, before it truncates** - with more than one class the switcher arrow shares the row, and "4B English" was cut even at 1280x800. It is measured in the page rather than with `fitText`, because Chalkboard and Comic Book letter the class name in faces wider than Andika. A long name with several classes ("Grade 4 English") still ends in "…"; tapping the name itself to switch classes would free the arrow's room, if that is ever wanted.

- **Desks: thirty, and a seventh column for a class of more than thirty (35 at most).** Six across and five deep fits most classes and looks right, so it stays exactly as it is; the seventh column appears on the right only when the roster passes thirty (or someone is still sitting in it), with no setting to find. The new desks are numbered 30-34 on the end of the seating list rather than re-numbering the grid seven wide, because re-numbering would have scrambled every saved seating chart; saves from before are padded with five empty desks on load. A 30-desk board was checked pixel-for-pixel against the old one. In a big class every desk is a seventh narrower and the names shrink with it (they are sized to the desk). Seat Class fills the seven columns bottom row first like the six; Pick Row picks from seven rows; Split by Rows can make seven groups; flip cards lay out seven wide past thirty (35 is exactly five full rows). **Known tight spot:** Pairs with 35 students makes 17 groups, which at 1024x640 squeezes the cards to six narrow columns, small names and a cut-off "Group…" title. Fine at 1280x800 and up.
- **Attendance: one button, one mark, nothing else.** An informal record for the teacher, not a register for the school, so it is kept as small as it can be. **Attendance** sits on the class's own row at the top of the panel. Tapping it turns the mode on, and then the only thing that answers is a desk: tap marks a student absent, tap again brings them back. Everything else on the panel stands down, as it does for Swap Seats, and the button stands down itself while flip cards or group cards cover the desks. Turning it off records the day, so a class with nobody away gets its check from on-then-off, and the button carries a green check for the rest of the day. The label never changes. **Absent is the only mark**: Late, Excused, a "Here now" pop-up for latecomers, a Done button and an "Everyone's here" button were all planned and dropped as clutter; a student who turns up late is just tapped back in with the mode on. An absent desk shows a **zzz** in place of the avatar and fades a little, the name still readable. One picture for everyone, because only 7 of the 20 sticker packs have a sleepy pose; the zzz was chosen over a thinner "dream cloud" because thick strokes survive a projector. **Absent lasts the day it was marked** and no longer: the record is keyed by local date, so tomorrow starts with everyone present (a date changing, not a timer). A student marked away ahead of time (below) simply starts that day away. For the rest of that day an absent student counts as not seated for everything that chooses students: Pick Student and Pick Row skip them, the flip deck doesn't deal them, Pick All and Everyone +1 leave them out, tapping their desk doesn't select them, and new groups and the Group Activity captions count only who is here. In "Continue with Last Groups" they stay on their team's card, faded with a small zzz, since the team is whole again tomorrow, but the group pickers pass them by and they get no stars when the group's points go out. **The record** is a month grid in Class Settings → Students → View / Edit Attendance, on the roster's header, and an Export to CSV. No print button: the browser can print. **It can be edited**: tap a square to mark a student away or bring them back, on any day - fixing a past mistake, or marking a trip next week. So every Monday to Friday gets a column, not only days attendance was taken (a missed day or a day ahead needs somewhere to tap); taken days read at full strength, the rest are quieter, today is highlighted, the weeks are ruled off, and the month arrows go forward without end. A square ignores a second tap for 0.7 s, because the board reads one touch as two. **Editing the record is not taking attendance**: a student marked away for Friday doesn't give Friday its green check, or one early absence would look like the whole class had been checked. Anything done in attendance mode at the board is taking it. The export has a column for every day that was taken or has someone marked away. The record lives in `ClassData.attendance` as date → absent ids, and `attendanceTaken` lists the days actually taken; saves from before that list read every recorded day as taken, which is what they meant, and any change writes the list out. Back up / Restore of all data was discussed and deferred.
- **Points**: floored at 0. Select students (tap desks), then +/− in the side panel. Selection is keyed by student id, which is what lets desks and flip cards share the same controls.
- **Flip Cards: one tap, one meaning, nothing timed.** A student comes up and taps a card; whoever it reveals has the turn, and the side panel's +/- go to them - no selecting a card first, because a tap on a face-up card is how it gets put away. The newest card glows amber; earlier face-up cards dim but stay readable, and stay up. Tapping a faded card gives it the turn back (a spelling bee's "try again") and fades whoever had it. **Only the glowing card can be put away**, and what that tap does is the one mode, **Flip Back** or **Discard**, and it sits on the flip toolbar rather than in settings because it changes what the tap means. Timed flip-back and set-aside were built and taken out: the teacher, not a clock, decides when a turn ends. So discarding a faded card takes two deliberate taps - wake it, then put it away - which is the protection a double-tap-to-discard would have given, without asking for a gesture the smart board fakes on its own. Putting away the active card leaves nobody active and +/- greyed rather than quietly handing the points back to someone who already had their turn. Reveal All is nobody's turn (every card at full strength, +/- greyed); Hide All only turns cards over - only a tap reaches the discard pile; Shuffle brings everything back. When the cards don't fill the last row, the short row goes on top: the bottom rows are the ones a small child can reach, so those are the ones kept full. The short row starts at the left, like a line of writing, so the empty spaces sit together at the top right (28 cards on six columns leaves the last two top-right slots empty). It was centred at first, which left a hole on each side and looked like cards were missing. The deal matches: cards land bottom row first, left to right, working up, so the short row is the last to arrive. Every card keeps its slot for the whole round: a discard leaves an empty space rather than letting the rest close up, so no card grows, shrinks or moves while the class is choosing. A discarded card flies over the board onto the pile, and the pile counts it when it lands. **Bonus cards** (off by default, a switch in flip card settings): Everyone +1, Jackpot +3 and Oops!. Pick a Friend was proposed and dropped - picking a friend is what the student at the board is already doing. Face down they look exactly like student cards, borrowing a random student's back colour, so nobody can hunt for them. The app picks how many (3-6, fewer for a small class) and the column count together so the board comes out as full rows; of the class sizes the chart can hold, only 7 and 10 can't. Everyone +1 lands the way Pick All then + would, class meter included; Jackpot waits on the toolbar ("Next card +3") and lands on the next student turned face up, stacking if two come first; Oops! does nothing. The only "bad" card is a dud because a flip is luck, and losing stars to luck isn't fair to the student who earned them. A bonus card is never anybody's turn: the glow stays where it was, and one tap puts a face-up bonus card away. Only a hand-flip pays out - Reveal All never does. Reveals are small (an icon pop or wobble, a short sparkle or wah-wah): the class goal is the one big celebration in the app, and a fanfare for every lucky card would wear it down. Turning bonus cards on or changing the kinds re-deals straight away if nobody has touched the deal yet; mid-round it waits for Shuffle. The side panel names the student ("Points go to Mulan") only on the flip cards, on one line that is always there so nothing jumps, and Pick All stands down so +/- never mean the whole room. Pick Student and Pick Row stand down too: the flip cards *are* the picker, and a pick behind the deck lit up desks nobody could see. Opening the cards clears a finished pick, and Flip Cards waits while a pick is still flashing. A card ignores taps for 0.7 s after it turns: smart boards read one touch as two, and in Discard mode that second tap would throw away the card it just revealed.
- **Class goal meter**: one shared total. Deductions affect a student's own tally but never pull the class meter backward — it's collective momentum, not a ledger. On reaching the goal: celebrate, then reset to 0 for the next goal. **The celebration keys off a "goals reached" counter, never off the total falling** — a reset and a correction are falls too, and Reset Class Goal used to throw the party (with whichever gif had last been decoded, which could be one the teacher had switched away from). **The meter can be corrected** with a "class points on the meter now" stepper in Pickers & Points, beside the goal stepper: a point that landed by mistake had no way back short of resetting the whole run. It's held to the goal and never opens the chest.
- **Two knobs on the goal, not one.** Stars convert to class points at a teacher-set rate, and the goal is counted in class points. A divisor doesn't change *pacing* — the goal alone already does, since "10 stars each, goal 8" is arithmetically "goal 80" — it changes the number the class looks at. 5/8 is a jar filling; 48/80 is a score. Leftover stars are banked, so awarding one at a time eventually counts for as much as awarding them all at once. Default is 1:1, so classes saved before this behave exactly as they did.
- **Resetting stars and resetting the meter are separate actions.** They were briefly one, on the grounds that both describe the same term — but that argues for making both easy, not for forcing a teacher who wants one to destroy the other.
- **Class Settings is two tabs, Students and Class.** It was one page grouped by row rather than meaning, and the roster - the part used most - got a row and a half of it on a big class. **Students** (opens first): Add a Student, then the roster, which takes all the height left, with **View / Edit Attendance**, Import CSV and Export CSV on its header (a CSV can still be dropped on the roster; the big drop box went, since on a board it was tapped anyway), and Unseat All beside Seat Students at the foot, because they are opposites. The attendance button is named for what it opens - the record - because attendance is *taken* on the seating chart with the side panel's button, not here. **Class**: New Class and Delete Class together at the top (the delete cover's note has room there; it used to run into New Class at 1920 wide), then the class name, the theme (marked as changing every class, since it does) and the avatars. The class name belongs to the class, not the students, which is why it moved here from beside Attendance. **Themes are picked from small drawings** - a strip of side panel and six desks - in each theme's real colours: the theme tokens also apply under `[data-theme-preview]`, so a change to a theme shows in its thumbnail by itself, plus a mark or two per theme (Comic Book's ink and dots, Chalkboard's wooden frame). **Avatars** shows the class's first ten avatars beside the button, so its effect is visible before it's tapped, and the button is **Student Avatars**, not Class Avatars: it sets every student's avatar, not something belonging to the class. What space is left on the Class tab stays empty rather than being filled for the sake of it. The window holds one height so switching tabs doesn't resize it.
- **Points settings live in Pickers & Points**, not Class Settings, with per-student editing in the roster (the only per-student surface). Class Settings is for the class's composition and appearance.
- **Flip-card picker**: slides over the desk grid rather than taking the full screen, so the side panel's point buttons and the goal meter stay live. Seated students only. Its own round, independent of Pick Student's history — setting a card aside *is* the no-repeat mechanism. Gender colouring on card backs is cosmetic (all students dealt), and flipping awards no points automatically.
- **Avatars**: Flaticon sticker packs (paid subscription), imported by `import-stickers.py` into `public/avatars/stickers/<theme>/<pose>.svg`. This replaced the Google Flow set and fixed the proportion drift outright — every sticker is drawn on the same 512×512 canvas, so nothing needs per-image CSS. A student with no avatar gets a character derived from their id, so a freshly imported class already looks varied.
- **Bulk avatar assignment**: Class Settings → Class → Student Avatars. Scope (everyone / boys / girls) × poses (mixed / same for all), then one tap applies; tapping the same character again re-rolls. Immediate rather than a confirm step — the undo is tapping something else.
- **Typeface**: Andika, app-wide. Chosen for its single-storey lowercase `a`, which is how the students are taught to write it; Comic Sans was rejected. Lexend was a close second. `lib/fitText.ts` measures in Andika because it picks one name size for a whole class — measuring in the wrong face would size every desk wrong.
- **Desk layout**: rounded top, square bottom. Homeroom number top-left, points badge top-right, both over the avatar's empty corners, leaving the whole bottom row to the name. One name size per class, computed from the longest name, so no student's desk reads smaller than the rest. **Names use their own colour, `--desk-name`, softer than the theme's text colour.** On a smartboard the names were the loudest thing on screen — big, bold and near-black at about 14:1 contrast, against 6:1 for the app's own button text — and read as glaring. Size was not the problem: a smaller name was still harsh, and a lighter weight is a big jump because Andika has only regular and bold, and thin strokes are the first thing a projector washes out. So the names stay bold and full size, and Light and Elementary each use a dark tinted from their own palette at about 9:1, well clear of the 7:1 readability bar. Dark (white on dark) and Comic Book (ink on purpose) keep their card text colour, and Chalkboard writes them in chalk white on a desk a shade deeper than the board, also about 9:1.
- **Themes**: five, chosen in Class Settings → Class — Light, Dark, Chalkboard, Elementary (the default until a teacher picks otherwise; only a pick in the picker is remembered, never the OS preference) and Comic Book. School Colors, built from the Yuteh crest, was removed at the teacher's request. Sky Blue made way for Chalkboard, in its slot in the picker: Light, Sky Blue and Elementary were all light and blue, so it was the one most like the others. A teacher who had a removed theme picked lands on Elementary. Three of the five are pure CSS-variable swaps and can stay that way. **Comic Book is the exception and is allowed to be**: what makes a page look printed is ink weight, a halftone lattice and hard shadows, none of which is a colour. So it carries a structural block in `index.css` alongside its tokens, under two rules that keep that from spreading. It only reaches for hooks that already exist — `data-slot` on the shadcn primitives, plus `data-ink` on the three app containers that had none (canvas, panel, desk) — and it never touches anything that affects layout: every outline is a `box-shadow` ring rather than a border, so switching themes cannot reflow the board. Bangers letters the chrome; student names stay in Andika, because an all-caps display face is the wrong thing for a roster of real names read across a room. Colour varies per desk through a rotating five, carried in a caption bar across the panel's top rather than a wash or a centred glow — both of those put saturated colour behind the student's name. Texture alternates between a halftone lattice and diagonal speed ruling, so neighbouring panels aren't printed the same way. **Chalkboard is the second exception, under the same two rules.** It came from the teacher's picture of a green classroom board with coloured chalk doodles and a wooden ledge. The board is chalkboard green with chalk dust (the splash screen's grain) and a wooden frame, drawn as an inset shadow inside the canvas's own padding so nothing moves. Desks are slate tiles a shade deeper than the board, outlined in soft white chalk: the side panel buttons' chalk, drawn a little heavier, with the homeroom number in the same chalk a step quieter than the name. The first version gave each desk one of five chalk colours on a diagonal, like Comic Book's caption bars, and on a real board thirty coloured outlines were a distraction from the names. It is a dark theme underneath, so the `dark:` variant covers it as well as Dark. The splash screen's chalk face (Cabin Sketch) letters the class name, panel headings and dialog titles; names stay in Andika for the same reason as Comic Book. Switched-on buttons are yellow chalk, dialogs get the wooden frame, and the flip deck and group cards get the dust through `data-ink="board"`. Flip card backs and group colours stay as they are in every theme.

- **Group Activity**: one button on the side panel, one modal, one screen. The modal is all buttons, no typing: how many groups (2–6), groups of (pairs, 3s, 4s, 5s), or split by boys/girls or rows, each captioned with what it makes of *this* class today ("7–8 each", "15 groups") and greyed out with the reason when it can't. Tapping one deals: the chips gather into a stack in the middle of the board and are dealt out one at a time into colour-banded cards, with the side panel's pickers and +/- standing down the way they do for Swap Seats — the timer stays live. Building groups by hand is deliberately not a mode (thirty students is sixty taps); fix-ups are tap a chip, then tap the card it belongs in — anywhere on the card, chips included. Dragging was built and taken out: on a touch board it fights with scrolling, and one way to do a thing is easier to teach than two. Chips are name and homeroom number only, no avatars, **one size for the whole class regardless of how many groups there are**, all the same width (set by the longest name, like the desks' one name size), so they line up in rows; a name past the cap shrinks inside its chip. Cards fit their contents and every card in a deal shares the tallest one's height, centred on the board, so empty space is around the cards rather than inside them. **The board never scrolls**: a planner knows every card's dimensions, so it picks the column count that fits at full-size chips, then tightens the card's chrome (three densities: padding, light size and button size give way in that order), and only when nothing fits does it shrink the chips a step (all of them, so one size for the class holds) and try again — names are what has to be read from the back of the room, so they are the last thing to give. The chrome is computed from the classes, not measured, since the plan decides which density renders; the frame is a real border, since a ring outside the card was clipped wherever it met the board's edge. Leftovers spread into existing groups rather than forming a group of one. Each card carries its own score, with the same +/- as the side panel. **Exit Group Activity** is the one way out: with nothing scored it just closes; with points on the board it asks whether they go out now or wait with the groups — handing thirty students their stars is not a side effect of leaving a screen. Points go where the toggle at the bottom of the modal says, remembered per class: a star per point to every member, or one class point per group point onto the goal meter. Choosing Class Goal with no goal switches the class goal on right there (greying the button out sent the teacher looking for a bug). Groups survive exit, so "Continue with Last Groups" picks the same teams up tomorrow, scores included if they were kept; anyone no longer seated drops out on the way; a shuffle keeps each card's identity by position so only the chips move. Colours are fixed across themes (they *are* the group's identity); everything else follows the theme, like Flip Cards. Names are Group 1, 2, 3 (or Boys and Girls) and can't be changed, so nothing in Group Activity asks anyone to type.
  - **Status.** Groups are for projects as much as games, so every card carries one of four states: Working, Help, Ready, Done. Ready and Done are deliberately different — Ready means a group has stopped and is waiting for the teacher, Done means there is nothing left to do. **The status colours the card, it is not a widget on the card.** The body and the points row both take a **strong** wash in the status colour and the band and frame keep the group's own colour, so scanning the room is "look for the red card", which is the red, amber and green cups on a group's table. The wash is deliberately heavy: the first attempt used a 16% tint and from the back of a room it was indistinguishable from the next card. Name chips sit on the plain card colour so they stay crisp on top of it. The small status chip in the band wears the status colour too, ringed in the band's own text colour so it still separates when a red group is the one asking for help — a faded chip said nothing at a distance and the chip and the body should say the same thing. Working has no wash at all: if every card were tinted the board would go one flat colour and a single red card would stop standing out. Help pulses a soft ring, and it is the only status that moves, because it is the only one asking for you now. Icons are Pencil, question mark, thumbs up and tick, chosen to be read without English — a raised hand turns to mush at small sizes and a flag has to be taught. Help, Ready and Done chime, switchable in the Group Activity modal. Statuses survive Exit and Continue and reset with a new deal.
  - **Group names cannot be edited.** They are Group 1, 2, 3 or Boys and Girls, and that is enough — a teacher who wants "Red Team" can say it. Renaming existed briefly and its field stretched from the name to the status chip, which mostly served as a way for a student to open a dialog by accident. Removing it removed the accident and a whole modal.
    - **The first version was a strip of four lights on the card and it was wrong twice over.** The lit light expanded in place, so its position moved from card to card and a board of fifteen never lined up; and the strip had a fixed 200px minimum the layout planner never reserved, so at fifteen groups it was clipped at both ends. Cards were optimised one at a time and never looked at fifteen side by side.
  - **Choosing a status is a panel low on the screen, not buttons on the card.** A card in the top row of a smartboard is two metres up and a small card would mean small buttons, which is worst for the shortest student. So the board blurs, the card flies down to a fixed spot and four large buttons sit beneath it in the bottom third. One row, left to right, in the order work goes: a two by two grid leaves a child guessing whether to read across or down. Every button wears its status colour, so the code is taught each time. **No close button and no instructions**: the group's current status is one of the four and is shown as already chosen, so a child who opened it by accident taps the lit one and nothing happens. Tapping outside closes it too. The panel is solid over a blurred board rather than frosted — the blur behind is what makes it feel lifted, and two translucent layers read as muddy on a projector that has already eaten the contrast.
  - **The points row is sized by the card's width, not by the density.** Density is chosen to make cards fit by *height*, so a board that went to eight columns could leave a card half as wide carrying a row built for a wide one — which is how the plus and minus ended up clipped at the card's edges. Widths and icon sizes now come from the card's width while heights still come from the density, so the planner's arithmetic stays true.
  - **Picking, during a group activity, reuses the side panel's two picker buttons.** Pick Row becomes **Pick Group** while the cards are up — a row and a group are both "a set of students", so the button keeps its meaning and only what counts as a set changes — and Pick Student flashes across the name chips instead of the desks. The pickers are a family and a teacher already looks in that box for them; putting one in the group toolbar would split them across two places and add a sixth control to a row that wraps. They were greyed out before because the desk picker lights up desks, which are behind the cards and so invisible here, so `useGroupPicker` is a separate hook at the same pace and with the same sounds, pointed at the cards. The winner is ringed in amber, the one colour that is never a group's and never a status. It honours the same Allow Repeats setting, tracked per activity and reset on a new deal, and a landed pick clears on a tap anywhere.
    - Two framer-motion traps here, both worth remembering: it will not clear an inline `box-shadow` it has set, so the winner's ring had to be a class or every past winner stayed ringed; and on an element with a `layoutId` the shared-layout pass writes an inline `opacity` that no utility class can beat, so a dimmed chip's opacity has to go through `animate`.
  - **The riffle belongs to the Shuffle button.** A first deal played it too, so making groups sounded like two sounds landing on top of each other; there is nothing to shuffle when the cards are being made for the first time.
  - **Reset Points** sits in the group toolbar, greyed until something has been scored, behind a confirm. It zeroes the scores and leaves the groups alone, for running the same teams through a second round.
  - **Settings and Swap Seats stand down during a group activity.** Both rearrange the class underneath a running activity, and Swap Seats acts on desks that are behind the cards. Swap Seats is out whenever the cards are up; Settings goes out when the board is locked, which is when students are the ones standing at it.
  - **Lock** is for students coming up to the board. It freezes what would destroy work: moving a name, New Groups, Shuffle, Exit and the side panel's button. **Points and status stay live**, because awarding a point for being on task mid activity is normal and unlocking to do it would be backwards. It is a courtesy lock, not a PIN.

- **Deployment**: pushes to `main` auto-deploy to https://a102032.github.io/seating/. The workflow curls the published URL afterwards and fails if it isn't serving the built bundle — Pages served the repo's raw `index.html` for several runs while every step reported green. `build-artifact.py` bundles a standalone copy for the Claude artifact preview.

## Known loose ends

- **Not every sticker pack can be filtered.** Only 7 of the 20 packs ship descriptive filenames (`sick`, `tired`, `broken-heart`); the other 13 are numbered (`owl-002`), so bulk assignment can't keep glum poses out of those and `absent` can't be detected for them either. Renaming them by hand is the only fix, and it's probably not worth it.
- **Andika ships only weights 400 and 700.** `font-semibold` renders identically to `font-bold` throughout the app. Nothing looks broken, but the type has one less step than the design assumed.
